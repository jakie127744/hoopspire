The NBL played no game from November 24 to December 2, 2025, and none from February 23 to March 3, 2026. Its 2026-27 schedule has no game from November 23 to December 1, 2026. Those are exactly the dates of FIBA's World Cup qualifying windows.

The league does not give a reason for its gaps, so this is a match of dates, not a stated cause. It is an exact match, three times, and it is the same pattern we found in [Brazil's NBB](/story/nbb-first-turn-no-club-plays-back-to-back).

The counts come from the published results for the 165 regular-season and 14 postseason games of 2025-26 and from the published 2026-27 regular-season schedule, 165 games with 54 played and 111 to come, as of November 5, 2026. Dates are Sydney time. The FIBA window dates were read from FIBA's own page and agree with a second published list. Games can be moved, so treat the counts as a snapshot.

## The windows and the gaps

| FIBA window | Dates | NBL regular-season and postseason games in the window | Last game before, first after |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | Nov 24 to Dec 2, 2025 | 0 | Nov 23, Dec 3 |
| 2nd | Feb 23 to Mar 3, 2026 | 0 | Feb 22, Mar 4 |
| 5th | Nov 23 to Dec 1, 2026 | 0 scheduled | Nov 22, Dec 2 |

The February 2026 pause came between the postseason's first game, on February 22, and its second, on March 4. The 6th window runs from February 22 to March 2, 2027. The listed 2026-27 regular season ends on February 14, and no postseason dates are listed yet.

The match has one limit. FIBA's 4th window ran from August 24 to September 1, 2026, and the NBL played three preseason games in it, on August 25, 27 and 29. So the pattern holds for the regular season and postseason, not for the preseason. The 3rd window, in late June and early July, falls well before the preseason starts.

## No two days in a row

In both seasons, every club has at least two days between games. There are no back-to-backs. The average gap between a club's consecutive games is 4.5 to 4.6 days in 2026-27 and 4.5 to 4.8 in 2025-26.

That is a very different calendar from the G League's. [We counted 236 back-to-backs there](/story/g-league-schedule-delaware-24-road-stops-maine-17), and the NBL's count is zero.

## How the season is laid out

The 2026-27 schedule has 165 games over 109 dates, from September 19 to February 14. Each of the 10 clubs plays 33. Fifteen pairs of clubs meet three times and 30 meet four times, which is 165 games in total. Five clubs have 17 home games and five have 16: Melbourne United, Perth, Sydney, Tasmania and Brisbane have 17.

Games lean on the weekend but spread across the week:

| Day | Games |
|---|---|
| Saturday | 39 |
| Sunday | 41 |
| Friday | 28 |
| Thursday | 26 |
| Wednesday | 23 |
| Monday | 5 |
| Tuesday | 3 |

Fifty-six of the 109 dates have two games and 53 have one.

The longest road run is Perth's six games. New Zealand and Illawarra have five. At home, Sydney and Brisbane each have a run of six, and Perth and New Zealand five. The longest wait between games for any club runs from 10 days (Phoenix) to 14 (Tasmania), and it includes the late-November pause.

The next longest break in the league-wide calendar is January 31 to February 4, when no club plays on three dates.

## What the numbers show, and what we read into them

The numbers show a calendar with a floor of two days between games, a 10-day gap each late November, and a break in 2026 that begins and ends on FIBA's window dates.

What it means is our reading. We do not know that the NBL built its schedule around the windows, and we are not claiming that. What we can say is that three gaps fit three windows to the day, and that the same is true of the NBB. Why a league would plan around the windows is not in the schedule, and we have not asked.

The test is the postseason. When the 2026-27 finals dates are published, check whether they avoid February 22 to March 2, 2027. If they do, that is a fourth match.
