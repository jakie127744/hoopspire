## How the list is built

**Game Score** is John Hollinger's one-line summary of a box score. It adds points, rebounds, assists, steals and blocks, and it takes away missed shots, missed free throws, turnovers and fouls.

**Availability.** A player with 65 games or more keeps his full score. Below that, the score is multiplied by the square root of games played over 65. The result is the Margin Score, and veterans are ranked by it alone. The pool is every current roster player with at least 20 games last season, 406 players.

## Two ways to reach 19

Part 6 split Game Score into a scoring part (points, plus 0.4 for each made shot, minus 0.7 for each attempt and 0.4 for each missed free throw) and everything else (rebounds, assists, steals and blocks, minus fouls and turnovers). The two always add up to Game Score.

- **Cunningham** has the biggest everything-else part in this group, 6.06 a game. He averaged 9.9 assists and 1.44 steals, both the most here.
- **Booker** has the smallest, 2.49. Almost all of his score is scoring.
- **Antetokounmpo** has the biggest scoring part, 18.71, by more than 1.6 over Jaylen Brown's 17.02.

## What this part does not say

**The top three are close.** Cunningham, Durant and Brown are separated by about 0.10 of Margin Score, 19.80, 19.73 and 19.70. Treat them as a tier.

**The cut for missed games is a rule, not a finding.** It treats 36 games as less evidence than 65. It says nothing about why Antetokounmpo missed games or how many he will play for Miami. If you believe he will be on the floor, his per-game rank, fourth, is the number to use.

**Game Score sees defense only through steals and blocks.** Whatever a defender's positioning is worth, it is not in the number beyond the steals and blocks it produces.
