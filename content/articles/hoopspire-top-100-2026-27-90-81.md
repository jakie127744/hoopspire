Three of the 10 players in this part of the countdown shot 67.4% true shooting or better last season. The 406 players in our pool shot 58.4% combined. Between them, [Deandre Ayton](/player/NBA/4278129), [Mark Williams](/player/NBA/4701232) and [Neemias Queta](/player/NBA/4397424) attempted nine threes all season.

They are not higher on this list because they did not do much else with the ball. Game Score rewards efficiency, but it rewards volume too, and a center who takes fewer than nine shots a night and makes most of them adds up to a solid number, not a big one.

This is Part 2 of 10, one a day, from No. 100 to No. 1. The method is the same as in [Part 1](/story/hoopspire-top-100-2026-27-100-91): each veteran's 2025-26 Game Score per game, cut for missed games below 65, and nothing moved by feel.

## How the list is built

**Game Score** is John Hollinger's one-line summary of a box score. It adds points, rebounds, assists, steals and blocks, and it takes away missed shots, missed free throws, turnovers and fouls.

**Availability.** A player with 65 games or more keeps his full score. Below that, the score is multiplied by the square root of games played over 65. The result is the Margin Score, and veterans are ranked by it alone. The pool is every current roster player with at least 20 games last season.

**Rookies** have no NBA box score. Three are placed on fit and projection, and each placement says why.

## The cases

**90. [Neemias Queta](/player/NBA/4397424), Celtics.** He averaged 10.2 points, 8.4 rebounds and 1.3 blocks in 25.3 minutes over 76 games, on 67.4% true shooting. Three of those rebounds a night came on the offensive glass. Game for game he ranks 105th. The 76 games lift him to 90th. His 2.8 fouls a game were the most in this group, and Game Score counts every one of them against him.

**89. [Brandin Podziemski](/player/NBA/4709138), Warriors.** He played all 82 games, the only player in this part to do it. His per-game production ranks 104th. He averaged 13.8 points, 5.1 rebounds and 3.7 assists, and made 37.1% of 412 threes. Like Collin Gillespie at 98 in Part 1, he is here because he was always there.

**88. [Ivica Zubac](/player/NBA/4017837), Pacers.** His 10.6 rebounds a game were the most in this group. He added 14.1 points on 62.2% true shooting and did not attempt a three. On production alone he is 71st. He played 48 games between the Clippers and Indiana, so the availability rule keeps 86% of his score.

**87. [Darius Garland](/player/NBA/4396907), Clippers.** Game for game he was the best player in this group, 61st in the pool. He led the ten in points (18.8) and assists (6.7) and made 39.6% of 308 threes. He also led them in turnovers, 2.9 a game. He played 45 games for Cleveland and the Clippers, the fewest of the ten, and keeps 83% of his score. Given full credit for his games, he would rank 54th.

**86. [Mark Williams](/player/NBA/4701232), Suns.** The best true shooting in this group, 68.0%, in the fewest minutes, 23.6 a night. He averaged 11.7 points and 8.0 rebounds and took one three all season. He played 60 games, close enough to 65 that he keeps 96% of his score.

**85. [Cameron Boozer](/player/NBA/5041935), Grizzlies (rookie).** Memphis took him third. At Duke last season he averaged 22.5 points, 10.2 rebounds and 4.1 assists over 38 games in 33.5 minutes, shot 55.6% from the field and made 39.1% of his threes on 3.6 attempts a game. The fit is open minutes. Only one Grizzlies player ranks above him in this countdown, [Jerami Grant](/player/NBA/2991070), two places ahead at 83, and no other Memphis player makes our top 100. The placement is our judgement, not a model output. Last season's top three picks finished 32nd ([Cooper Flagg](/player/NBA/5041939), first pick), 135th ([Dylan Harper](/player/NBA/5037871), second) and 69th ([VJ Edgecombe](/player/NBA/5124612), third) in our model. We put Boozer behind Edgecombe's finish, and ahead of Darius Acuff Jr., the seventh pick, whom we placed at 95.

**84. [Alex Sarr](/player/NBA/5160992), Wizards.** He blocked 2.0 shots a game, the most in this group, and scored 16.3 points with 7.4 rebounds. He is 21. The two numbers that hold him down are 54.4% true shooting, the lowest of the ten, and 48 games. Per game he ranks 68th.

**83. [Jerami Grant](/player/NBA/2991070), Grizzlies.** He scored 18.6 points a game for Portland on 60.8% true shooting. He got to the line 5.6 times a game, the most in this group, and made 81.4%. He hit 2.4 threes a game at 38.9%. His 3.5 rebounds are the thin part of the line. He played 57 games and keeps 94% of his score.

**82. [Deandre Ayton](/player/NBA/4278129), Wizards.** He made 67.1% of his shots from the field for the Lakers and did not attempt a three. He averaged 12.5 points and 8.0 rebounds in 72 games. He also had the fewest assists in this group, 0.8 a game, and the lowest free throw percentage, 64.5%. Game for game he ranks 100th. His games move him up to 82nd, two places ahead of his new teammate Sarr.

**81. [Josh Hart](/player/NBA/3062679), Knicks.** A guard by ESPN's listing, he averaged 7.4 rebounds, 4.8 assists and 12.0 points. He made 41.3% of 242 threes and had the most steals per game in this group, 1.1, just ahead of Podziemski. He played 66 games, one over the line, so his score is not cut.

## What this part does not say

**Game Score favours the ball.** A center who sets screens and finishes at the rim creates shots that show up in a teammate's line, not his own. Queta, Williams and Ayton are the players in this part most likely to be ranked too low.

**The cut for missed games is the biggest single factor here.** Four of the nine veterans in this part reached 65 games. Given full credit for their games, Garland would rank 54th, Sarr 59th and Zubac 60th.

**Team context is last season's.** Ayton and Grant play for new teams this season, and Garland and Zubac were traded during the last one. Their numbers come from their old roles.
