[LeBron James](/player/NBA/1966) turns 42 in December. Last season, his 23rd, he averaged 20.9 points, 6.1 rebounds and 7.2 assists in 60 games, and his Margin Score of 17.34 makes him No. 21 in this countdown, the highest of the ten players in Part 8. He is one of four first overall picks in this part, with [Karl-Anthony Towns](/player/NBA/3136195), [Zion Williamson](/player/NBA/4395628) and [Paolo Banchero](/player/NBA/4432573).

Part 7 ended with a check to run: how many of these ten play enough games to keep their full score? Seven of them do. Only James (60 games), Williamson (62) and [Pascal Siakam](/player/NBA/3149673) (62) fall under the 65-game line, and they keep 96%, 98% and 98% of their scores. Every one of the ten ranks higher on this list than he does game for game, because players above them in the per-game order played fewer games and fell below them. The ten are separated by 0.90 of Margin Score, 16.43 at the bottom and 17.34 at the top.

This is Part 8 of 10, from No. 100 to No. 1. The method has not changed since [Part 1](/story/hoopspire-top-100-2026-27-100-91): each veteran's 2025-26 Game Score per game, cut for missed games below 65, and nothing moved by feel. [Part 7](/story/hoopspire-top-100-2026-27-40-31) covered No. 40 to 31.

## How the list is built

**Game Score** is John Hollinger's one-line summary of a box score. It adds points, rebounds, assists, steals and blocks, and it takes away missed shots, missed free throws, turnovers and fouls.

**Availability.** A player with 65 games or more keeps his full score. Below that, the score is multiplied by the square root of games played over 65. The result is the Margin Score, and veterans are ranked by it alone. The pool is every current roster player with at least 20 games last season, 406 players.

**Rookies** have no NBA box score, so they are placed on fit and projection. All three have already appeared, and this part has none.

## Two ways to reach 17

Part 6 split Game Score into two parts: the scoring part (points, plus 0.4 for each made shot, minus 0.7 for each attempt and 0.4 for each missed free throw) and everything else (rebounds, assists, steals and blocks, minus fouls and turnovers). The two always add up to Game Score.

Williamson and Barnes are three places apart and come at it from opposite ends.

- **Williamson** has the biggest scoring part in this group, 14.16 a game, and the smallest everything-else part, 3.17. He shot 60.0% from the field and 64.4% true shooting, both the best here.
- **Barnes** has the smallest scoring part, 10.84, and the biggest everything-else part, 5.90. He blocked 1.45 shots a game, the most in the part, with 5.9 assists and 1.43 steals.

James, at 5.23 everything else, is the only one of the four first overall picks above five. His 7.2 assists are the most in the part.

## What this part does not say

**The middle of this part is a near tie.** Murphy and Banchero are about 0.01 apart, and Adebayo and Barnes about 0.03, 16.773 and 16.746. Do not read much into the order inside those pairs.

**Age is not in the method.** James's place rests on his 2025-26 box score at 41. The list does not project what any player will do next season, and it does not discount anyone for age.

**Game Score sees defense only through steals and blocks.** Whatever a defender's positioning is worth, it is not in the number beyond the steals and blocks it produces.
