## How the list is built

**Game Score** is John Hollinger's one-line summary of a box score. It adds points, rebounds, assists, steals and blocks, and it takes away missed shots, missed free throws, turnovers and fouls.

**Availability.** A player with 65 games or more keeps his full score. Below that, the score is multiplied by the square root of games played over 65. The result is the Margin Score, and veterans are ranked by it alone. The pool is every current roster player with at least 20 games last season.

**Rookies** have no NBA box score, so they are placed on fit and projection. All three have already appeared in this countdown, and this part has none.

## What the score is made of

Part 5 ended with a question: how many of the players in a part are ranked on something other than points? Game Score can be split to answer it. The **scoring part** is points plus credit for made shots, minus debits for missed shots and missed free throws. **Everything else** is rebounds, steals, assists and blocks, minus fouls and turnovers. The two add up to the Game Score per game, and they match the published figure to the second decimal for all ten.

## What this part does not say

**The spread is wider than in Part 5, and the order inside is still thin.** Ingram and Bane are 0.06 apart, and Fox, DeRozan and Giddey are within 0.08 of each other. The top two are 0.24 clear of Ball.

**The cut for missed games is a rule, not a finding.** It treats 38 games as less evidence than 65, and it says nothing about why the games were missed or what Butler will play next season. The 19th-place figure is arithmetic on his per-game rate, not a projection.

**Game Score sees defense only through steals and blocks.** Whatever a defender's positioning is worth, it is not in the number beyond the steals and blocks it produces.
