The G League's Tip-Off Tournament has 217 games, and every one of them is between two clubs from the same division. There is no exception. A Central club plays Central clubs, a West club plays West clubs, and it goes on that way for 14 games each.

The regular season looks different. Only 155 of its 527 games, 29.4%, are inside a division. Put the two phases together and the 744-game schedule splits exactly in half: 372 games within divisions and 372 across them.

That split decides who plays whom, and it makes records in the standings less comparable than they look. The counts below are built from the published 2026-27 schedule for all 31 clubs as it stood on November 5, 2026, with divisions taken from the league's own alignment: Central (8 clubs), East (8), South (8) and West (7). Games can move, so treat them as a snapshot. The same schedule is the base of our [look at road stops and back-to-backs](/story/g-league-schedule-delaware-24-road-stops-maine-17).

## Fourteen games, one division

Every club plays 14 Tip-Off games, and in three of the four divisions the arithmetic is tidy. Seven rivals, two games each, one at home and one away.

| Division | Clubs | Tip-Off games per club | How often each pair meets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Central | 8 | 14 | Twice, every pair |
| East | 8 | 14 | Twice, every pair |
| South | 8 | 14 | Twice, every pair |
| West | 7 | 14 | Twice for 14 pairs, three times for 7 pairs |

The West has only six rivals per club, so 14 games cannot be split evenly. Seven of its 21 pairings meet a third time. Every one of the 105 pairings inside a division appears somewhere in the Tip-Off schedule, and nothing else does.

## Then the map opens, partly

From December 27 the schedule opens up. The regular season has 527 games, and 372 of them are across divisions. That sounds like a wide mix, but it still leaves most pairings unplayed.

Of the 360 possible pairings between clubs in different divisions, 144 meet during the season and 216 never do. Across all 465 pairings in the league, 249 meet and 216 do not, which is 46.4% that never play.

The number of opponents a club faces runs from 14 to 21 out of a possible 30. The median is 16 and the mean 16.1.

| Group | Clubs | Different opponents per club | Games against own division |
|---|---|---|---|
| West | 7 | 14 | 25 to 28 |
| East | 8 | 15 | 26 to 29 |
| South, excluding Laketown and Osceola | 6 | 16 | 22 to 24 |
| Central, excluding Iowa and Sioux Falls | 6 | 17 | 22 to 23 |
| Central: Iowa and Sioux Falls | 2 | 20 | 16 |
| South: Laketown and Osceola | 2 | 21 | 15 |

Four clubs stand apart. Iowa and Sioux Falls in the Central, and Laketown and Osceola in the South, play 15 or 16 games against their own division, where their divisional neighbors play 22 to 24. After the Tip-Off Tournament those four have only one or two divisional games left, while their neighbors have eight to ten. Across the whole season they face 20 or 21 different opponents.

Repeat meetings are common. Of the 249 pairings that do meet, 10 meet once, 109 meet twice, 16 meet three times, 102 meet four times and 12 meet five times. So 114 of the 249 pairings meet four or five times.

## What the numbers show, and what we read into them

The counts show three facts. The Tip-Off Tournament never leaves the division. Across the season a club meets about 16 of its 30 rivals, and 46.4% of all pairings never play. And the pool size is not equal: 14 different opponents for every West club, 21 for Laketown and Osceola.

What it means is our reading, and we have not rated any club. If divisions are close in strength, an uneven pool is harmless. If they are not, a record earned against 14 opponents and a record earned against 21 are not the same record. The standings will show both as wins and losses. Nothing has been played, so there is no way yet to say which case this is.

The test will be cheap once games exist. Compare the clubs that meet each other across divisions. If the West's record against the South tracks its record in the Tip-Off Tournament, the pools are similar. If it does not, the unequal pools are a story. Until then, a record in this league is a statement about a set of opponents, and the set is different for each club.
