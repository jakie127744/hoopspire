The WNBA playoff field is done: Minnesota, Golden State, Las Vegas, Atlanta, Indiana, New York, Washington and Dallas are the eight teams in, and the gap to ninth place is too large for anyone else to reach. What is not done is the order they finish in, and with the regular season closing this week, three of the eight seeds are still genuinely in play.

**The No. 1 seed is not decided.** Minnesota (32-10) leads Golden State (31-11) by exactly one game, with each team playing twice more before the postseason. Minnesota holds the tiebreaker regardless — the Lynx won the season series 3-1 — but the Valkyries are the hotter team by a wide margin, on a six-game win streak against Minnesota's one, and their two remaining games are against Portland and the Los Angeles Sparks, both lottery teams. Minnesota's two remaining games are both against Indiana, a team fighting for playoff position of its own. If Golden State wins out and Minnesota loses both, Golden State finishes 33-11 to Minnesota's 32-12 and takes the top seed outright.

**The 4 and 5 seeds are one game apart.** Atlanta (28-14) has a single game left, at New York, on September 24. Indiana (27-15) has two, both against Minnesota. If Atlanta wins its last game, the 4 seed is Atlanta's regardless of what Indiana does. If Atlanta loses and Indiana sweeps Minnesota, Indiana finishes 29-15 to Atlanta's 28-15 — same number of losses, more wins — and passes Atlanta for the 4 seed. It is a real scenario, not a long shot: Minnesota may have little left to play for in those two games once its own seeding is settled.

**Seeds 6, 7 and 8 are a flat tie.** New York, Washington and Dallas are all 26-16, and the schedule is not treating them equally. New York has one game left, at home against a playoff-caliber Atlanta team. Washington has two, against the Connecticut Sun (10-32) and the Chicago Sky (15-27), the two worst records left on anyone's schedule. Dallas has one, against the Seattle Storm, who at 8-35 have the worst record in the league. Washington and Dallas are favored to win out against that competition; New York has to beat a good team just to hold its spot.

None of that changes the first-round pairings in principle — 1 plays 8, 2 plays 7, 3 plays 6, 4 plays 5, best-of-three, and the bracket does not reseed after that round. It changes who is actually standing in each slot when the first round tips off September 27.

**The predictions, for what a seed and a scoring margin are worth this early.** On the numbers as they stand — point differential, current form, and nothing else — Minnesota and Golden State are the two best teams in the league by a clear margin over the rest of the field, and the numbers favor both advancing out of the first round regardless of which of them ends up the 1 seed. Because the bracket does not reseed, that puts them on opposite sides of it: the 1-8 winner meets the 4-5 winner in one semifinal, the 2-7 winner meets the 3-6 winner in the other, which keeps Minnesota and Golden State apart until the final, not before it. Las Vegas, third at plus-221, is the pick in the 3-6 matchup regardless of which of New York, Washington or Dallas ends up occupying the 6 seed; Atlanta's plus-273 differential is the best in the league outside the top two, which makes the Dream the favorite over Indiana in a 4-5 matchup even with the seeds this close.

The numbers point toward Minnesota and Golden State both reaching the final, which is the matchup the regular season has already been building toward for a month — decided, on this evidence, by whichever of the two is actually playing better in October rather than by which of them finishes with the extra home game in September. Semifinals start October 4, best-of-five; the final starts October 17, best-of-seven. Whether Minnesota gets there as the 1 seed or the 2 is a question this week's schedule will answer before the bracket does.
