The gold medal game between South Korea and Japan at the Asian Games was 59 points lower than their group game a week earlier. It finished 67-57, 124 points in all, against 183 in Korea's 100-83 win on 13 September. Three-pointers account for three of the missing 59. Two-pointers account for 28. Free throws account for 28.

Both games are in the official Asian Games results, so the game figures below come from the tournament's own box scores, not from match reports.

## Where the points went

| Points scored, both teams | Group game | Gold medal game | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| From two-pointers | 84 | 56 | -28 |
| From three-pointers | 57 | 54 | -3 |
| From free throws | 42 | 14 | -28 |
| Total | 183 | 124 | -59 |

The teams did not slow down. They took 133 field goal attempts in the group game and 135 in the final, and turned the ball over 22 times in each. The shots simply stopped going in: 61 made in the group game, 46 in the final.

| Both teams combined | Group game | Gold medal game |
|---|---:|---:|
| Two-pointers made | 42 of 83 (50.6%) | 28 of 80 (35.0%) |
| Three-pointers made | 19 of 50 (38.0%) | 18 of 55 (32.7%) |
| Free throws made | 42 of 49 (85.7%) | 14 of 29 (48.3%) |
| Team fouls | 48 | 36 |

Free throw attempts fell from 49 to 29 while fouls fell from 48 to 36. That fits fewer trips to the line, but the box score cannot say why. It does not record which fouls were called, how the games were officiated or who was guarding whom, so this piece does not claim a cause.

## Korea's threes did not change

The popular reading of the final is that Korea got hot from three. The box score supports a narrower version of that. Korea shot 10 of 26 (38.5%) from three in the final and 9 of 23 (39.1%) in the group game, almost the same rate.

What changed for Korea was inside the arc and at the line. Korea scored 50 points from twos and 23 from free throws in the group game, and 28 and 9 in the final, a drop of 36 points between them. Its three-point points went up by three. Korea won the second game with a worse field goal percentage, 38.7% against 50.7%, and Japan's was worse still, 30.1% against 40.9%.

The third quarter is where Korea's threes did land. Korea scored 27 points in it, on 9 of 14 shooting overall and 6 of 8 from three. Outside that quarter Korea went 15 of 48 from the field (31.2%) and 4 of 18 from three. One quarter with eight three-point attempts is a small sample. The official five-minute scoring shows Korea outscoring Japan 16-11 in the first five minutes of the quarter and 11-8 in the last five, so the lead was built across the period.

## Lee Hyun-jung carried the outside shooting

Lee Hyun-jung scored 27 of Korea's 67 points, 40.3%, on 8-of-12 shooting. He made five of Korea's 10 threes on eight tries. The rest of the roster shot 5 of 18 (27.8%) from three. He also took 18 rebounds, 14 of them defensive, in 34 minutes 26 seconds. Lee Jung-hyun added 14 points and seven assists.

Korea's outside shooting in this game came mostly from one player. That is a fact about this game, and the box score cannot say whether it repeats.

## Japan's two scorers went cold

The change is sharpest on the Japan side. Kotetsu Kurokawa scored 21 points on 8-of-16 shooting in the group game and five on 2-of-14 in the final, going 0 for 6 from three. Alex Kirk scored 19 on 5-of-11 shooting, with 9 of 10 free throws, in the group game and five on 2-of-9 in the final. Together they went 13 of 27 in the first game and 4 of 23 in the second. Their points fell from 40 to 10.

Others filled part of the gap. Ren Kanechika scored 13 in the final against nine in the group game and Atsuya Ogawa 12 against four. Japan's total still fell by 26 points, and 14 of them were free throws: Japan made 19 of 21 in the group game and five of 14 in the final.

Here is how those numbers travel from the B.League, where both played last season. Hoopspire's B.League ledger has Kurokawa shooting 43.1% from the field over 57 games in 2025-26 and Kirk 63.1% over 67 games. At those rates their 27 shots in the group game would produce about 13.8 makes, and they made 13. Their 23 shots in the final would produce about 11.7, and they made four. So Japan's top two scorers shot close to their domestic rates in the loss by 17 and far below them in the loss by 10.

Two cautions apply. A club rate does not transfer unchanged to a national-team opponent, so this measures a gap and not a reason. And two games is a small sample, 50 shots between two players. The KBL ledger has no entry for Lee Hyun-jung, so there is no domestic baseline for Korea's best player in the final, and this comparison covers Japan only.

## What Japan had and did not use

Japan out-rebounded Korea 54 to 43, with 21 offensive rebounds to nine. It took 73 shots to Korea's 62 and scored 11 second-chance points (points after its own offensive rebounds) to Korea's six. It also led in points in the paint, 24 to 20, and in bench scoring, 26 to 15. Korea led in fast-break points, seven to three, and led for 25 minutes 42 seconds of the game to Japan's 9:09.

Estimated with the standard formula (field goal attempts, plus 0.44 times free throw attempts, minus offensive rebounds, plus turnovers), Japan used about 71 possessions and Korea about 69. That puts Japan's output near 80 points per 100 possessions and Korea's near 98. Possession counts are not in the official box score, so treat those as estimates. The gap between them is a shooting gap: Japan had more shots and more second chances, and made 22 of 73.

## What it means

Korea won the final by 10, and the official box splits that margin cleanly. Both teams scored 28 points from two-pointers. Korea scored six more from threes (30 to 24) and four more from the line (nine to five).

The game as a whole was the same size as the first one in shots and turnovers and much smaller in scoring, and that is mostly the two-point and free throw columns, not the threes. Both teams lost 14 points at the line between the games, Korea from 23 to nine and Japan from 19 to five.

My reading is that the two teams cancelled each other inside the arc, and that Korea's edge was Lee Hyun-jung's five threes and a small advantage at the line. Two games cannot show whether that is a pattern, and the box score cannot show whether Korea changed how it defended Kurokawa and Kirk between the two meetings. That is the question worth asking the coaches, and it is the number to check first the next time these two teams meet.

The win ended South Korea's 12-year wait for men's basketball gold at the Asian Games.
