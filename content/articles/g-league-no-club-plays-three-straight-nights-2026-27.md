Across 744 games and 31 clubs, no G League team plays on three consecutive dates in 2026-27. No team plays twice in one day. No team plays four games in five days. And every one of the 31 clubs reaches, at some point, a stretch of exactly four games in seven days, which is the most the schedule allows.

The limits are tight, and the league holds them for every club. That is worth knowing, because the usual way to describe a schedule is by its back-to-backs. The back-to-back is the ceiling here. The real difference between a light schedule and a heavy one sits just under it.

The counts come from the published 2026-27 schedule for all 31 clubs as it stood on November 5, 2026. Dates are US Eastern, and a stretch is counted by its first game. Games can move, so treat them as a snapshot. This piece builds on [our count of road stops and back-to-backs](/story/g-league-schedule-delaware-24-road-stops-maine-17), which measured travel. Here the measure is how many games fall in a short window.

## The limits

Three rules show up in every club's schedule:

- At most two consecutive dates with a game. Zero clubs play three in a row.
- Never more than one game on a date. Zero double-days.
- At most four games in any seven dates. All 31 clubs hit four, and none hits five.

Two straight dates is the longest run any club faces. What varies is how often a club has three games in four days, and four in seven.

## Three in four, and four in seven

Counting every stretch of three games inside four dates, the league has 160 in all. Thirty-seven fall in the Tip-Off Tournament and 123 in the regular season. The median club has five. The range is wide.

| Club | Three games in four dates | Four games in seven dates |
|---|---|---|
| Osceola Magic | 9 | 14 |
| Iowa Wolves | 8 | 17 |
| Windy City Bulls | 8 | 16 |
| Oklahoma City Blue | 7 | 18 |
| Stockton Kings | 2 | 6 |
| Austin Spurs | 2 | 7 |
| Rio Grande Valley Vipers | 0 | 5 |

The table shows the clubs with the most of each measure and the clubs with the fewest. Osceola has the most three-in-four stretches, nine. Oklahoma City has the most four-in-seven, 18. Rio Grande Valley has the fewest of both: none of the first and five of the second. The median club has 11 four-in-seven stretches, and the league has 350 of them in all.

This does not line up with back-to-backs. Our earlier count had Greensboro with the most back-to-backs, 12, yet it has seven four-in-seven stretches, among the lowest in the league. Osceola has eight back-to-backs, one above the league median of seven, but the highest three-in-four count. A club can be light on one measure and heavy on the other.

## When the games fall

The calendar adds a second layer. League-wide, the busiest week of the regular season has 46 games (the week of January 18) and the lightest has 29 (February 15). Twelve of the 13 regular-season weeks, from December 28 to March 22, sit between 33 and 46 games:

The Tip-Off Tournament weeks run lighter. Its busiest, the week of November 16, has 39 games.

## What the numbers show, and what we read into them

The numbers show a schedule with hard limits and an uneven middle. Everybody has the same ceiling of two nights in a row and four games in seven, but the number of heavy stretches runs from 0 to 9 for three-in-four, and from 5 to 18 for four-in-seven.

What we read into that is a caution about shorthand. Calling a schedule heavy because of its back-to-backs misses the stretches that actually pack games together. A fair test needs results, and nothing has been played yet. When it has, the question is whether clubs do worse in the games that end their four-in-seven stretches. The sample is large enough to ask: 160 three-in-four stretches and 350 four-in-seven.
