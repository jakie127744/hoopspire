[Franz Wagner](/player/NBA/4566434) produced like the 43rd-best player in the NBA last season, game for game. He played 34 games. That is why he opens this countdown at No. 93 and not in the 40s, and it is the rule this whole list runs on: what a player did in 2025-26, and how often he was on the floor to do it.

This is the first of 10 parts, one a day, from No. 100 to No. 1. It is our own ranking, built from the 2025-26 regular-season box scores of every player on an NBA roster today.

## How the list is built

Every veteran gets one number, which we call the Margin Score. It has two parts.

**Game Score per game.** Game Score is John Hollinger's one-line summary of a box score. It adds points, rebounds, assists, steals and blocks, and it takes away missed shots, missed free throws, turnovers and fouls. A night of 20 points on 20 shots scores lower than 20 points on 12. We use each player's season totals, divided by his games.

**Availability.** A player who reached 65 games, the minimum the league sets for its season awards, keeps his full score. Below that, the score is multiplied by the square root of games played over 65. At 50 games a player keeps 88% of his score. At 34 games he keeps 72%. The square root is our choice. It makes missed games cost something without letting one injury erase a season.

The pool is 406 players with at least 20 games last season. Only 175 of them reached 65 games. Veterans are ranked strictly by the Margin Score, and nobody was moved by feel.

**Rookies** have no NBA box score, so they cannot be scored. They are our dark horses: three of them are placed in the list on fit and projection, and each placement says why.

## The cases

**100. [Bennedict Mathurin](/player/NBA/4683634), Pelicans.** He averaged 17.6 points in 54 games for Indiana and the Clippers, and 6.2 free throw attempts a game carried much of it. He made 86.9% of them. He shot 31.5% on 238 threes. Take the free throws away and there is not much efficiency left in the line.

**99. [Anthony Black](/player/NBA/4712849), Magic.** Fifteen points, 3.7 assists and 1.4 steals in 64 games. His 54.9% true shooting is the second-lowest in this group of ten, and 33.3% from three on 300 attempts is the number that holds him down.

**98. [Collin Gillespie](/player/NBA/4278585), Suns.** Game for game he ranks 113th. He is here because he played 80 games. He made 232 threes at 40.1%, 2.9 a game, and turned the ball over 1.6 times a night while averaging 4.6 assists.

**97. [Collin Sexton](/player/NBA/4277811), Lakers.** He scored 15.4 points in 23.7 minutes, split between Charlotte and Chicago, with 61.5% true shooting. That is starter scoring from a bench role. The rest of the line is thin: 2.3 rebounds and 3.3 assists.

**96. [Shaedon Sharpe](/player/NBA/4914336), Trail Blazers.** He scored more than anyone else in this group, 20.8 points a game, but did it on 54.6% true shooting, the lowest of the ten, with 2.9 turnovers. He played 50 games. The volume is real. The efficiency is not there yet.

**95. [Darius Acuff Jr.](/player/NBA/5142620), Kings (rookie).** The seventh pick averaged 23.5 points and 6.4 assists at Arkansas last season, over 36 games and 35.1 minutes, and made 44.0% of his threes. The fit is the case. No player on Sacramento's current roster ranks inside our top 100: the best of them, [Maxime Raynaud](/player/NBA/4898371), is 104th in the model. The ball and the minutes are available to him from the first night. The honest range is wide. Of last season's top five picks, three finished inside our top 100 as rookies ([Cooper Flagg](/player/NBA/5041939) 32nd, [Kon Knueppel](/player/NBA/5061575) 52nd, [VJ Edgecombe](/player/NBA/5124612) 69th) and two did not. We put Acuff at the back end of that range because he was picked seventh, not in the top five.

**94. [Kel'el Ware](/player/NBA/5105623), Bucks.** Nine rebounds in 22.1 minutes, 1.1 blocks, and 39.5% on 233 threes, from a center. He turned it over 0.8 times a game, the fewest in this group. The limit is minutes: he played the fewest of the ten.

**93. [Franz Wagner](/player/NBA/4566434), Magic.** On production alone he is 43rd. He averaged 20.6 points on 58.8% true shooting. He played 34 of 82 games, so the availability rule keeps 72% of his score. If he plays a full season at that level, he will not be anywhere near this part of next year's list.

**92. [Jaren Jackson Jr.](/player/NBA/4277961), Jazz.** He averaged 19.4 points and 1.4 blocks, the most blocks in this group, over 48 games split between Memphis and Utah. His per-game production ranks 75th. The missed games drop him to 92nd.

**91. [Jaden McDaniels](/player/NBA/4431671), Timberwolves.** He shot 41.2% from three on 250 attempts, the best rate of the ten, with 61.1% true shooting, 1.1 steals and 1.0 blocks in 73 games. He played 31.7 minutes a night on a modest box score. When a coach gives that many minutes to that line, the value is usually somewhere the box score does not look, most often defense.

## What this list does not say

**Most defense is invisible here.** Steals and blocks count. Guarding the other team's best scorer, rotating on time and keeping a man off the glass do not appear in a box score. Players like McDaniels are the ones this method is most likely to rank too low.

**Some stars are not on it.** Players who appeared in fewer than 20 games last season are outside the pool. That leaves out [Jayson Tatum](/player/NBA/4065648) (16 games), [Trae Young](/player/NBA/4277905) (15), [Tyrese Haliburton](/player/NBA/4396993), [Kyrie Irving](/player/NBA/6442) and [Damian Lillard](/player/NBA/6606), who did not appear. The list ranks last season, as we said it would. It does not predict who will be best this season.

**Team context is last season's.** Mathurin, Sexton and Jackson Jr. changed teams during the season, and several players in this countdown play for new teams this year. Their numbers come from their old roles.
