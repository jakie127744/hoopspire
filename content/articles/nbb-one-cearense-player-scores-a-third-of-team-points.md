Basquete Cearense has scored 263 points in four games, and one player, Dupree, has scored 87 of them. That is 33.1% of the team's total. The second scorer, Gabriel, has added enough that the two together account for 52.1%, more than half of everything the club has scored. Cearense is 0-4.

The number is the highest of the 18 NBB clubs, and the next highest is 28.3%. The median club's top scorer provides 19.4% of its points. This piece looks at how far one scorer's share goes toward explaining a club's start.

The counts come from the league's published 2026-27 player statistics and standings through games of November 4, 2026. Each player's points per game, multiplied by games played, gives that player's total. Adding the players on a club reproduces that club's listed points exactly, for all 18 clubs. The sample is small: each club has played between three and six games, and names are shown as the league lists them.

## How concentrated each club's scoring is

Across the league the top scorer's share runs from 15.4% (UNIFACISA) to 33.1% (Cearense). The median is 19.4%.

The first four rows are the four clubs most reliant on one scorer, each at 24% or more. Their combined record is 3-14. The last three rows are three of the five clubs with at least four wins.

All five of those clubs, Minas Storm, Mogi, UNIFACISA, Pinheiros and Flamengo, have a top scorer between 15.4% and 21.5% of their points. None of them is among the four most dependent clubs.

## What the numbers show, and what we read into them

The numbers show a link between heavy reliance on one scorer and a poor start, across a small sample. The four most concentrated offences have won three of 17 games, and the five best records all belong to clubs where no scorer is above 21.5%.

What it means is our reading, and the direction could run either way. A club that struggles to score might lean on its best player because nobody else is producing. A club that leans on one player might struggle because defences can focus on that player. Three to six games cannot separate those.
