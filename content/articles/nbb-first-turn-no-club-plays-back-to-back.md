In the NBB's 2026-27 first turn, 153 games are scheduled and not one club plays on consecutive days. The shortest wait between two games for any club is two days. A gap of two days means one day off, and 100 of the 288 gaps between a club's consecutive games are that short. Leaving out the league-wide pause, the average gap is 3.9 days.

The G League, by contrast, has 236 back-to-backs in a season, and the same sort of count in Brazil is zero. [We counted them in the G League](/story/g-league-schedule-delaware-24-road-stops-maine-17). The NBB turn is a different kind of schedule: 17 games per club, more space between them, and an unusual hole in the middle.

The counts below come from the league's published first-turn schedule and its results through November 4, 2026. The turn is a single round robin of 18 clubs, so every pairing meets once: 18 clubs, 17 games each, 153 in all. Dates are as listed by the league. Games can move, so treat the counts as a snapshot.

## One turn, one meeting per pair

The first turn runs from October 17 to December 29, over 57 distinct game dates. Forty-two games have been played, which leaves 111. Home games are nearly balanced. Nine clubs host nine games and nine host eight, because 17 games cannot split evenly.

| Home games in the first turn | Clubs |
|---|---|
| 9 | 9 |
| 8 | 9 |

Games are spread across the week, not packed onto weekends. Wednesday has the most games with 27, then Thursday with 25, Monday and Tuesday with 23 each, Saturday with 20, Sunday with 18 and Friday with 17.

## The rest between games

Across the 18 clubs there are 288 intervals between consecutive games. None is shorter than two days.

| Days between games | Intervals |
|---|---|

| 4 | 38 |
| 5 | 32 |
| 6 to 9 | 38 |
| 10 to 13 | 24 |
| 18 to 19 | 4 |

Only 100 of the 288 gaps are the two-day minimum. The long end of the table is the pause, and it is worth a closer look.

## The 10-day hole

The last game before the pause is on Sunday, November 22, a five-game round: Cearense v Flamengo, São José v Corinthians, Brasília v Pato, Mogi v Paulistano and Bauru v Ceisc/União Corinthians. The next game anywhere is on December 2. No club plays on any of the nine dates from November 23 to December 1.

Those dates are the same as the fifth window of FIBA's 2027 World Cup qualifiers, which runs from November 23 to December 1. The NBB schedule does not give a reason for the gap, so we are reporting a match of dates, not a cause.

The pause does not cost every club the same. Ten clubs played on November 22, and their waits run from 10 to 19 days:

- Corinthians waits 19 days, from November 22 to December 11.
- Mogi, Flamengo and Brasília wait 18, from November 22 to December 10.
- Paulistano waits 11, until December 3.
- Bauru, São José, Ceisc/União Corinthians, Cearense and Pato wait 10, until December 2.

The league lists two clubs with Corinthians in the name: Corinthians, and Ceisc/União Corinthians. The 19-day wait belongs to the first.

The eight clubs that played earlier wait between 11 and 13 days. UNIFACISA is the longest of them at 13, from November 19 to December 2.

The only other gap of more than two days is between December 23 and December 27, when no club plays.

## Stretches on the road

Home and away runs are mostly short. Pinheiros and Paulistano each have a run of five consecutive away games, the longest in the turn. No other club has more than four. At home, six clubs have a stand of four games, which is the longest: Corinthians, Flamengo, São José, Botafogo, Paulistano and Brasília.

## What the numbers show, and what we read into them

The numbers show a turn built with a hard minimum rest and a long hole in the middle. They do not show whether that is deliberate. The two-day floor looks like a rule of the schedule, because 288 intervals with no exception is not an accident. The hole matches an international window, but that is a match, not an announcement.

What it means for results is our reading, and nothing has been played to test it. Four clubs wait 18 or 19 days, and their first games back, on December 10 and 11, are the place to look.
