If you plan to punt points, cross Jaylen Brown off your board. He ranks 50th of 150 in a nine-category pool and 128th once points stop counting. Donovan Clingan goes the other way, from 27th to 11th. Draft him where the punt board puts him, not where a standard cheat sheet does.

This column is the next in the punt series. Punting means giving up a category on purpose and building around the rest. **Format:** nine-category head-to-head, where you win a week by taking five of nine categories. Punt points and you need five of the remaining eight: rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, threes, field goal percentage, free throw percentage and turnovers.

**Pool:** the 150 players with the most nine-category value among the 247 NBA players who played at least 30 games and 20 minutes a night in 2025-26. Every figure is a 2025-26 per-game average at that player's own minutes, built from box score totals. A player traded during the season counts his games for both teams. Nothing here is a projection of 2026-27.

## How points are scored

Points are a count. The pool averages 16.83 points a game, with a standard deviation of 5.65. A player's z-score in points is how many of those standard deviations he sits above or below 16.83. Luka Dončić, at 33.5 a game, is 2.95 above, the best in the pool. Luke Kornet, at 6.5, is 1.83 below, the lowest.

When you punt points, that score is removed from every player's total. The scorers lose the most. Everyone who does his work somewhere else gains.

## What points travel with

This is the part that surprises people. Across the 150 players, the points z-score correlates -0.74 with the turnovers z-score. Turnovers are scored so that fewer is better, so the negative sign means the high scorers turn the ball over more. The 14 players who fall 30 places or more averaged 23.4 points and 2.7 turnovers a game. The pool averaged 16.8 and 1.9.

That is why the scorers fall so far. In nine-cat their points pay for their turnovers. Punt points and the payment is gone, but the turnovers are still there.

Points also travel with assists (0.54), threes (0.47) and free throws (0.36). They have almost no link to rebounds (0.07), blocks (-0.12) or field goal percentage (-0.04). So the players who rise do their work in those three columns: big men who rebound and block, and guards who steal.

## How the punt ranking is built

Each player has a z-score in each category, which measures how far he sits from the pool average in standard deviations. To build the punt ranking I subtracted each player's points z-score from his nine-category total and re-ranked the 150. I did not rebuild the pool without points, so a player just outside the 150 could rank differently in a true punt draft. Read the ranks as a guide to direction and size, not as exact draft positions.

## What barely changes

The first five picks are the same five players in the same order: Nikola Jokić, Victor Wembanyama, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, Kawhi Leonard and Tyrese Maxey. Each of them is strong enough elsewhere that losing points barely shows.

Seven of the top 10 hold, and 16 of the top 20. The exits are Donovan Mitchell (7th to 12th), Jamal Murray (8th to 13th) and Lauri Markkanen (10th to 15th). Dončić slips from 6th to 10th, which is still a first-round pick, because his 8.3 assists and 1.6 steals go on counting. Scottie Barnes, Kevin Porter Jr. and Chet Holmgren move into the top 10, from 12th, 14th and 15th. Forty-one of the top 50 hold.

## The minutes assumption

Minutes work differently once points are gone. Take a hypothetical wing, not a real player, who scores 0.55 points, grabs 0.14 rebounds and turns it over 0.09 times a minute.

- At 26 minutes: 14.3 points (z-score -0.45), 3.64 rebounds (-0.98) and 2.34 turnovers (-0.58).
- At 34 minutes: 18.7 points (+0.33), 4.76 rebounds (-0.47) and 3.06 turnovers (-1.52).

Going from 26 to 34 minutes adds 0.78 to his points z-score, which a standard league counts and a punt-points league does not. It adds 0.51 from rebounds and takes away 0.93 from turnovers, in both. Counting only those two categories, and holding the other seven fixed, the extra eight minutes cost a punt-points manager 0.42. In a standard league they add 0.36.

That is a two-category sketch. His assists, steals and threes also scale with minutes, and for some players they would outweigh it. The point is where to look. When camp news says a scorer's minutes are going up, a punt-points manager should check his turnover rate before celebrating. When it says a big man's minutes are going up, pay attention: his rebounds, blocks and field goal percentage scale with it and none of them were ever in doubt.

## What this does not say

This is 2025-26 data, one season of it, and it says nothing about 2026-27 roles. Antetokounmpo played 36 games and Wagner 34, and per-game ranks ignore that. The pool was fixed before points were removed, and a player's rank in a real punt draft depends on who else is available.

The finding that holds is the shape of it. The top five do not care. Below them the board splits: the scorers who pay for their points with turnovers fall 30 places or more, and the big men and defensive guards move up. If you punt points, commit to it. Give up the scorers you would have taken in rounds four to six, and build around rebounds, blocks, steals and turnovers.
