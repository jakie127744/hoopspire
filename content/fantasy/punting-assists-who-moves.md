Josh Giddey ranks 61st of 150 in a nine-category pool. Remove assists from the scoring and he ranks 146th. Cade Cunningham goes from 19th to 71st, a fall of 52 places. If you plan to punt assists, do not pay draft-day prices for either of them.

This column follows the earlier ones on blocks, free throws and turnovers. It is about punting assists. Punting means giving up the category on purpose and building around the other eight. **Format:** nine-category head-to-head, where you win a week by taking five of nine categories. Punt assists and you need five of the remaining eight.

**Pool:** the same 150 players as the nine-cat top 50 and the earlier columns, taken from the 240 NBA players with at least 30 games and 20 minutes a night in 2025-26, then the top 150 by nine-category value. Every figure is a 2025-26 per-game average at that player's own minutes, from ESPN. Nothing here is a projection of 2026-27.

## How assists are spread

The pool averages 3.74 assists a game with a standard deviation of 1.99. Only five of the 150 players average 8.0 or more, and four of those five are in the nine-category top 20. The ten best players in the nine-category ranking average 5.76 assists a game.

Assists travel with scoring and turnovers. Across the 150 players, assists correlate 0.55 with points and 0.76 with turnovers. They correlate -0.30 with blocks and -0.04 with rebounds. That is why punting assists pushes the draft toward centers. The players who pass the least tend to block more.

## How the punt ranking is built

Each player has a z-score in each category, which measures how many standard deviations he sits from the pool average. To build the punt ranking I subtracted each player's assist z-score from his nine-category total and re-ranked the 150. I did not rebuild the pool without assists, so a player just outside the 150 could rank differently in a true punt draft. Read the ranks as a guide to direction and size, not as exact draft positions.

## What barely changes

The top holds. Nine of the ten best players stay in the top ten, and 16 of the top 20 and 41 of the top 50 hold. Victor Wembanyama moves from 2nd to 1st, Kawhi Leonard from 4th to 2nd, and Nikola Jokić, who averages 10.7 assists, drops from 1st to 4th.

My reading is scale. The best players are so far ahead in points, rebounds and shooting that removing one category does not close the gap.

The top ten does change at its edge. Jamal Murray falls from 8th to 20th, and Chet Holmgren rises from 16th to 7th.

## The minutes assumption

An assist total is a per-minute rate multiplied by a guess about minutes. Here is the arithmetic for a hypothetical guard, not a real player, who records 0.19 assists a minute.

- At 28 minutes a game: 5.32 assists, which is 1.58 above the pool average and about 0.79 standard deviations.
- At 34 minutes a game: 6.46 assists, which is 2.72 above the pool average and about 1.37 standard deviations.

The same player is worth 0.57 standard deviations more in this category at 34 minutes than at 28. If the minutes are a guess, the value of the category is a guess too, and it grows with every minute a passer plays. Those are the players a punt build gives up.

## What this does not say

This is 2025-26 data, one season of it, and it says nothing about 2026-27 roles. A player who moves to a new team, or into the starting lineup, changes his assist total, and this list changes with it. The pool was fixed before assists were removed, and a player's rank in a real punt draft depends on who else is available. Punting is a choice for a team you have already built, not a rule for every draft.

The finding that holds is the size of the effect. At the top of the board it is small. In the middle of the board, dropping one category moves the eight players in the second table by 30 to 85 places.
