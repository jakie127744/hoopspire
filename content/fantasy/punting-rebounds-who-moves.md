Shai Gilgeous-Alexander ranks third of 150 in a nine-category pool. Remove rebounds from the scoring and he ranks first, ahead of Nikola Jokić. Rudy Gobert goes the other way, from 79th to 147th. If you plan to punt rebounds, take Gilgeous-Alexander with the first pick and let Gobert go to someone else.

This column is the next in the punt series. Punting means giving up a category on purpose and building around the other eight. **Format:** nine-category head-to-head, where you win a week by taking five of nine categories. Punt rebounds and you need five of the remaining eight.

**Pool:** the 150 players with the most nine-category value among the 247 NBA players who played at least 30 games and 20 minutes a night in 2025-26. Every figure is a 2025-26 per-game average at that player's own minutes, built from box score totals. A player traded during the season counts his games for both teams. Nothing here is a projection of 2026-27.

## How rebounds are scored

Rebounds are a count. The pool averages 5.80 rebounds a game, with a standard deviation of 2.20. A player's z-score in rebounds is how many of those standard deviations he sits above or below 5.80. Jokić, at 12.9 a game, is 3.23 above, the best in the pool. Garland, at 2.4, is 1.55 below, the lowest.

When you punt rebounds, that score is removed from every player's total. The big men lose the most. The guards who barely rebound gain the most.

Rebounds travel with two other categories. Across the 150 players, the rebounds z-score correlates 0.52 with field goal impact and 0.46 with blocks. It runs against threes (-0.45) and free throw impact (-0.44). So a punt-rebounds team usually gives up blocks and field goal percentage as well, whether it means to or not. That leaves six categories, and you need five of them: points, assists, steals, threes, free throws and turnovers.

## How the punt ranking is built

Each player has a z-score in each category, which measures how far he sits from the pool average in standard deviations. To build the punt ranking I subtracted each player's rebounds z-score from his nine-category total and re-ranked the 150. I did not rebuild the pool without rebounds, so a player just outside the 150 could rank differently in a true punt draft. Read the ranks as a guide to direction and size, not as exact draft positions.

## What barely changes

The first six picks are the same six players: Gilgeous-Alexander, Tyrese Maxey, Jokić, Victor Wembanyama, Kawhi Leonard and Luka Dončić. Jokić drops from first to third and Wembanyama from second to fourth. Both lose one of their strongest categories, and what is left still keeps them in the first round.

Nine of the top 10 hold. Lauri Markkanen slips from 10th to 13th, and Stephen Curry comes in, from 11th to 8th. Sixteen of the top 20 and 41 of the top 50 hold.

He shot 52.6% from the line last season, and his free throw impact sits 3.40 standard deviations below the pool average, the lowest mark in any of his categories. Donovan Clingan is the pick to think hardest about. He is a third-round pick in nine-cat, 27th, and falls to 85th. If your league drafts him early, let it.

Twenty players fall 30 places or more, and 16 of them are centers. They averaged 9.5 rebounds a game, against 5.80 for the pool.

## The minutes assumption

Minutes work differently for the two groups when you punt. Take two hypothetical players, not real ones.

A guard who grabs 0.10 rebounds a minute:

- At 24 minutes: 2.4 rebounds, a rebounds z-score of -1.55.
- At 32 minutes: 3.2 rebounds, a rebounds z-score of -1.18.

A center who grabs 0.35 rebounds a minute:

- At 28 minutes: 9.8 rebounds, a z-score of 1.82.
- At 34 minutes: 11.9 rebounds, a z-score of 2.78.

In a standard league, six more minutes add 0.96 to the center's value from rebounds alone. In a punt-rebounds league they add nothing there, and the extra minutes count only in his other categories. For a center those are usually field goal percentage and blocks, the two that travel with rebounds and that you have probably written off already. So when camp news says a center's minutes are going up, a punt-rebounds manager should shrug. When it says a guard's minutes are going up, pay attention: his points, assists, steals and threes all scale with them, and his rebounds no longer cost you anything.

## What this does not say

This is 2025-26 data, one season of it, and it says nothing about 2026-27 roles. Antetokounmpo played 36 games and Garland 45. The pool was fixed before rebounds were removed, and a player's rank in a real punt draft depends on who else is available.

The finding that holds is the shape of it. The top six do not care. Below them the board splits cleanly: 16 centers fall 30 places or more, and the guards who make threes and free throws take their places. If you punt rebounds, commit to it. Give up blocks and field goal percentage along with it, and draft for the five categories the guards win.
