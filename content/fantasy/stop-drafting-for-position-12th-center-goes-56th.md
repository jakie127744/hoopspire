Here is the draft-day rule I would give you: take the best player on your board, every round, until the last few picks. Do not reach for a center because the centers look thin. They are thin. It does not matter.

The thinness is real. The 12th-best guard in the pool ranks 25th overall. The 12th-best forward ranks 33rd. The 12th-best center ranks 56th. If every team in a 12-team league needs a center, it looks as if the centers will run out around the fifth round and somebody will be stuck.

Nobody gets stuck. I built the league and checked.

**Format:** head-to-head nine-category or roto, 10 to 14 teams. **Pool:** the same 150 players as the last week of columns, the most nine-category value among the 247 NBA players who played at least 30 games and 20 minutes a night in 2025-26. Every figure is a 2025-26 per-game value at that player's own minutes. Nothing here is a projection of 2026-27. **Positions** are the single position ESPN lists for each player: guard, forward or center. Real leagues give many players two or three positions, which only makes my point stronger.

## How I priced position

A player's **nine-category value** is the sum of his z-scores in the nine categories: how many standard deviations he sits from the pool average in each. Zero is an average player in this pool.

To price position, you compare each player with the best player still available at his position once every team has filled its starting lineup. That fallback is called **replacement level**. A guard worth +1.00 when replacement guards are worth -2.06 is 3.06 better than what you could get later. If centers were scarce, replacement centers would be much worse than replacement guards, and every center's price would jump.

I filled 12 starting lineups in a common shape: point guard, shooting guard, guard, small forward, power forward, forward, center and three utility slots. That is 36 guard spots, 36 forward spots, 12 center spots and 36 that take anyone. I gave each slot to the best player left who could fill it.

## The answer: replacement level is flat

| Setup | Replacement guard | Replacement forward | Replacement center | Biggest move |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 12 teams, one center slot | -2.06 | -2.08 | -2.09 | 2 places |
| 12 teams, two center slots | -2.06 | -2.08 | -2.09 | 2 places |
| 10 teams, one center slot | -1.52 | -1.44 | -1.45 | 4 places |
| 14 teams, two center slots | -2.62 | -2.53 | -2.63 | 4 places |

In the standard 12-team league the three replacement levels sit within 0.03 of each other. Re-rank the whole pool by value over replacement and the biggest move is two places: [LaMelo Ball](/player/NBA/4432816) drops from 40th to 42nd and [Alex Sarr](/player/NBA/5160992) climbs from 43rd to 41st.

The reason is in the counts. Take the 120 most valuable players, enough to fill 12 starting lineups, and 27 of them are centers. The league needs 12. Even with two center slots it needs 24. Drafting on value alone already takes more centers than the slots require, so the center slot never decides a pick.

## A worked example

Two hypothetical players are on the board in the fourth round. Guard A is worth +1.00. Center B is worth +0.90.

- Guard A over replacement: 1.00 minus -2.06 is 3.06.
- Center B over replacement: 0.90 minus -2.09 is 2.99.

Take Guard A. The position premium for the center is 0.03, smaller than the 0.10 gap in their values. In this pool, a center has to be within about 0.03 of a guard before position breaks the tie.

## What to do

- **Standard 10-to-14-team nine-category draft:** rank on value and draft down the list. Check positions only in the last three or four rounds, when one open slot can decide between two near-equal players.
- **Do not trade up for a center** because the run has started. The run ends; the centers behind it do not.
- **If your league starts three centers, or has 16 teams,** rerun the check. I tested up to 28 center starters, and the slot still did not bind; past that I have not looked.

The thing that is scarce at center is not the position, it is the categories. The blocks column showed how few players give you real blocks without costing you threes. Draft for those numbers if you need them, and let the position take care of itself.
