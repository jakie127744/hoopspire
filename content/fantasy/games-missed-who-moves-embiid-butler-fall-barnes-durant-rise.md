If you rank your draft board by per-game value, you are treating every player as if he plays every game. For [Joel Embiid](/player/NBA/3059318) that is a 38-game season, and it is the difference between 20th and 31st. [Jimmy Butler](/player/NBA/6430) goes from 22nd to 34th, [Kevin Porter Jr.](/player/NBA/4397140) from 14th to 24th and [Paul George](/player/NBA/4251) from 30th to 38th. On the other side, [Scottie Barnes](/player/NBA/4433134) goes from 12th to 9th and [Kevin Durant](/player/NBA/3202) from 16th to 11th.

This is the column after the punting series, and it asks a different question: what is a missed game worth? **Format:** season-long nine-category leagues, head-to-head or roto, where the games a player misses are games your roster fills with someone else. **Pool:** the 150 players with the most nine-category value among the 247 NBA players who played at least 30 games and 20 minutes a night in 2025-26. Every figure is a 2025-26 per-game average at that player's own minutes, and a player traded during the season counts his games for both teams. Nothing here is a projection of 2026-27. My assumption is that each player plays the same number of games, at the same per-game rate, that he did last season. I have not tested whether last season's games predict next season's.

## How I priced the games

A **z-score** measures how many standard deviations a player sits from the pool average in one category. A player's **value per game** is the sum of his nine z-scores, so zero is an average player in this pool and Embiid's +2.59 is far above that. The per-game ranking uses that number alone.

To price games I assumed an 82-game season and filled every missed game with a stand-in. Two stand-ins bracket the answer:

- **Pool average.** The stand-in is worth zero. A player's priced value is his value per game times games played over 82. This is the generous reading.
- **Pool floor.** The stand-in is worth -2.84, the value of the 150th player. A player's priced value is his games times his value per game, plus his missed games times -2.84, all over 82. A waiver pickup comes from outside the 150. This is the harsh reading, and the one I would use for a thin wire.

A worked example with a hypothetical player worth +3.00 a game over 41 games: at the pool average he is worth 3.00 times 41 over 82, or +1.50. At the pool floor he is worth (3.00 times 41, plus -2.84 times 41) over 82, or +0.08. Same player, same rate, and the answer depends on who plays the other 41 games.

## What barely changes

The top does not care. The same five players stay in the top five under both readings, and [Tyrese Maxey](/player/NBA/4431678) and [Kawhi Leonard](/player/NBA/6450) swap places. Nine of the top 10 hold at the pool average and eight at the floor. In the top 20, 18 hold at the average and 16 at the floor. In the top 50, 49 hold at the average and 44 at the floor. The games move players around inside the board. The biggest drops are the twelve players below, and the biggest rises are the nine after them.

## What to do

1. **Rank by the floor column in a league with a thin wire and no free injured-list slots.** That is where a missed game hands the slot to a player from outside the top 150. Drop the twelve above by the amounts in the table, and move the nine up.
2. **Use the average column where you have a deep bench or free injured-list slots.** There a missed game costs less than the floor reading charges, and the case for the twelve is stronger.
3. **If you want the 38-game players anyway, buy them as stashes, not as foundations.** Embiid, Butler and Porter Jr. each played 38 games last season. Do not take any of them with a pick you need to get 82 games of production from.
4. **Do not ask this column whether a player will be healthy.** It cannot say. It says what last season's absences cost if they repeat.

## What this column does not say

**It uses one season.** I have not tested whether a player's games last year predict his games next year, and a player who missed 44 games may play 78 in 2026-27. Read the table as the price if the absence repeats.

**The pool hides the worst cases.** A player had to play 30 games to be in the 247, so anyone who missed more than 52 games is not on this board at all. The true cost of absence is larger than the table shows.

**The floor reading is one choice of stand-in.** A real league has a waiver wire with players above and below -2.84. The two readings are bookends.

**Rank is not draft position.** A player's rank here is among 150 in this pool, and the pool was built from per-game values, not rebuilt after the games were priced.
