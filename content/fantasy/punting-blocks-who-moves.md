If you plan to punt blocks, you can still take [Victor Wembanyama](/player/NBA/5104157) with the ninth pick. Do not take a center for his blocks. [Rudy Gobert](/player/NBA/3032976) goes from 79th to 140th. [Alex Sarr](/player/NBA/5160992) goes from 43rd to 128th and [Evan Mobley](/player/NBA/4432158) from 45th to 112th. On the other side, [Jalen Brunson](/player/NBA/3934672) climbs from 48th to 28th, and he blocks 0.1 shots a game, so the category you gave up was never his.

This is the ninth and last column in the punt series. Punting means giving up a category on purpose and building around the rest. **Format:** nine-category head-to-head, where you win a week by taking five of nine categories. Punt blocks and you need five of the remaining eight: points, rebounds, assists, steals, threes, field goal percentage, free throw percentage and turnovers.

**Pool:** the 150 players with the most nine-category value among the 247 NBA players who played at least 30 games and 20 minutes a night in 2025-26. Every figure is a 2025-26 per-game average at that player's own minutes, built from box score totals. A player traded during the season counts his games for both teams. Nothing here is a projection of 2026-27.

## How blocks are scored

Blocks are rare, and one player owns them. The pool averages 0.65 blocks a game, with a standard deviation of 0.46. A player's z-score in blocks is how many of those standard deviations he sits above or below 0.65. Wembanyama, at 3.1 a game, is 5.28 above, far and away the best in the pool. The lowest score is 1.19 below, and 40 of the 150 players block 0.3 a game or fewer.

When you punt blocks, every player's score in the category is removed from his total. Wembanyama loses the most of anyone. He still finishes ninth, because the rest of his line carries him: 25.0 points and 11.5 rebounds.

## What blocks travel with

Blocks come packaged. Across the 150 players, the blocks z-score correlates 0.46 with rebounds and 0.31 with field goal percentage. The shot blockers are the players who sit in the paint and finish at the rim.

The price is on the other side of the ledger. Blocks correlate -0.32 with threes, -0.30 with assists, -0.28 with free throw percentage and -0.22 with steals. The centers who block a lot do little of the work guards do, so when the blocks stop counting, what they do not do starts to show. The 14 players who fall 30 places or more averaged 1.5 blocks, 7.3 rebounds, 1.9 assists and 1.1 threes a game. The pool averages 3.8 assists and 1.7 threes.

## How the punt ranking is built

Each player has a z-score in each category, which measures how far he sits from the pool average in standard deviations. To build the punt ranking I subtracted each player's blocks z-score from his nine-category total and re-ranked the 150. I did not rebuild the pool without blocks, so a player just outside the 150 could rank differently in a true punt draft. Read the ranks as a guide to direction and size, not as exact draft positions.

Forty-three of the top 50 hold.

## The minutes assumption

Minutes work differently once blocks are gone. Take a hypothetical center, not a real player, who blocks 0.06 shots and grabs 0.27 rebounds every minute he plays.

- At 22 minutes: 1.32 blocks (z-score +1.44) and 5.94 rebounds (+0.06).
- At 30 minutes: 1.80 blocks (+2.48) and 8.10 rebounds (+1.05).

Going from 22 to 30 minutes adds 1.04 to his blocks z-score and 0.99 to his rebounds z-score. A standard league counts both, for a gain of 2.03. A punt-blocks league counts only the rebounds, so the same eight minutes are worth 0.99, about half as much.

That is a two-category sketch. His points and field goal percentage also move with minutes. The point is where to look. When camp news says a big man is getting more minutes, a punt-blocks manager should ask what he does besides block. When it says a guard is getting more, the news is pure upside, because nothing he does was ever in the punted column.

## What this does not say

This is 2025-26 data, one season of it, and it says nothing about 2026-27 roles. Sarr and Jackson played 48 games and Porziņģis 32, and per-game ranks ignore that. The pool was fixed before blocks were removed, and a player's rank in a real punt draft depends on who else is available.

The finding that holds is the shape of it. Wembanyama is so good everywhere that even a 5.28 blocks z-score is not what makes him a first-round pick. Below him the board splits: the centers who were drafted for their blocks fall 30 places or more, and the guards who do everything else move up. If you punt blocks, commit to it. Give up the rim protectors you would have taken in rounds three to six, and spend those picks on guards who score, shoot and pass.
