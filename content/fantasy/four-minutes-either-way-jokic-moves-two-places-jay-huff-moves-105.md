Here is where to spend your prep time this week: not on the first round. Spend it on the minutes of the players you will take between picks 50 and 100. That is where a wrong guess about playing time costs you the most, and the numbers below show how much.

Our first column, [Draft the minutes, not the names](/story/draft-the-minutes-not-the-names), ended with a step: sort your board by how far each projection moves if your minutes guess is wrong. Today I ran that step on real players.

**Format:** head-to-head nine-category or roto. **Pool:** the same 150 players as the recent columns, the most nine-category value among the NBA players who played at least 30 games and 20 minutes a night in 2025-26.

## How I measured it

Every projection is a rate times a minutes number. So I kept each player's 2025-26 per-minute rates fixed in all nine categories and changed only the minutes:

- **Low case:** his 2025-26 minutes per game, minus four.
- **High case:** his 2025-26 minutes per game, plus four.

Counting stats scale with minutes. So do the two percentage categories, because they are scored as impact, which grows with the number of shots. His value is the sum of his nine z-scores, each one how far he sits above or below the pool average in a category. I then re-ranked that one player against the other 149 at their real 2025-26 numbers. The pool averages stay where they are, so only the one player moves.

Two limits. First, a player whose minutes go up does not always keep his per-minute rates. This assumes he does. Second, the pool has 150 players, so 150th is the floor and first is the ceiling. Fifty-one players hit the floor in the low case, so the swings at the bottom of the board are smaller than they would be in a bigger pool.

## The top barely moves

| Rank now | Player | Minutes | At minus four | At plus four |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | [Nikola Jokić](/player/NBA/3112335) | 34.8 | 3 | 1 |
| 2 | [Victor Wembanyama](/player/NBA/5104157) | 29.2 | 5 | 1 |
| 3 | [Shai Gilgeous-Alexander](/player/NBA/4278073) | 33.2 | 5 | 1 |
| 4 | [Kawhi Leonard](/player/NBA/6450) | 32.1 | 6 | 3 |

None of the top six moves more than four places. Jokić moves two. Four minutes of value is small next to their lead over the players behind them.

It opens up fast after that. [Donovan Mitchell](/player/NBA/3908809), seventh, runs from sixth to 19th. [Jamal Murray](/player/NBA/3936299), eighth, runs from seventh to 22nd.

**Decision:** in the first round, take the player you want. Do not burn time on his minutes.

## The middle is where the guess is the pick

| Rank band | Median places moved, low case to high case |
|---|---:|
| 1 to 10 | 4 |
| 11 to 25 | 28 |
| 26 to 50 | 49 |
| 51 to 75 | 75 |
| 76 to 100 | 85 |
| 101 to 125 | 81 |
| 126 to 150 | 58 |

In the 51 to 100 band, 34 of the 50 players move 75 places or more across the eight-minute window. The 126 to 150 band moves less only because those players hit the floor of the pool.

The biggest swings in the whole pool:

- [Jay Huff](/player/NBA/4065731), 90th at 21.0 minutes. Minus four puts him 150th. Plus four puts him 45th. That is 105 places.
- [Isaiah Hartenstein](/player/NBA/4222252), 86th at 24.2 minutes. From 142nd to 44th, 98 places.
- [Daniel Gafford](/player/NBA/4278049), 104th at 21.7 minutes. From 150th to 53rd, 97 places.

**Decision:** for every player you are thinking about between picks 50 and 100, write down one minutes number before the draft. If you cannot defend it, you do not know where he belongs, and neither does the ranking you are reading.

## The best minutes bet in the pool

Each player's value rises by a fixed amount for each extra minute. I call that his value per minute. Only four players in the pool add more per minute than [Kel'el Ware](/player/NBA/5105623): Wembanyama, Jokić, Gilgeous-Alexander and Leonard.

Ware ranks 47th at 22.1 minutes a game. At 18.1 he would be 107th. At 26.1 he would be 20th. That is the shape of a minutes bet: a modest pick if his minutes stay flat, a top-20 return if they grow by four.

I do not know his 2026-27 minutes, and nothing in this column says what they will be. What the numbers say is how much the answer is worth.

## Do it for your own board

Use a hypothetical player so the arithmetic stays visible:

- Player C plays 24 minutes and adds 0.45 of nine-category value per minute.
- At 20 minutes he loses 4 x 0.45 = 1.8 of value.
- At 28 minutes he gains 1.8.

In our pool, the 60th player losing 1.8 of value would fall to 113th. That is 53 places, more than four rounds of a 12-team draft, from a four-minute guess.

**What I would do:** lock in your first round from the list. Then take your board from 50 to 100 and put a minutes number next to every name. Where you are unsure, draft the player whose value per minute is high, because if the minutes come, he pays you most.
