Draft Giannis Antetokounmpo like you drafted him last year. Draft Bam Adebayo one full round later than his name recognition says to. That is the call, and here is the arithmetic behind it.

**Format:** this applies to both nine-category and points leagues, since it is about raw minutes and touches, not category punting. I am pricing 2026-27 value, not re-running last season's numbers.

## The assumption, stated up front

Miami traded for Antetokounmpo in July, and he now shares a frontcourt with Adebayo, a fellow big who was the team's offensive engine before the trade. Beat reporters covering the Heat's September minicamp (Eastern Herald, September 12) say coach Erik Spoelstra plans to stagger the two rather than play them heavy minutes together at the same time, so one of them is anchoring the center spot whenever the other sits, with Bobby Portis absorbing the rest of the frontcourt bench minutes. Camp opens officially September 29 and nothing is final until real games are played. Treat what follows as a projection built on a reported plan, not a locked rotation.

I am assuming Antetokounmpo at 30 minutes a game and Adebayo at 29, both down from where they sat last season (28.9 and 32.4). If the stagger plan does not survive contact with the regular season, the numbers below move with it, and I will revisit them once real games are on the board.

## The two per-36 rates

Both lines are 2025-26 averages, so treat the sample size honestly: Antetokounmpo's is from an injury-shortened 36-game season with Milwaukee, Adebayo's from a full 73-game season with Miami.

## What the stagger plan does to each line

Multiply each per-36 rate by the new minutes assumption, and the two players move in opposite directions relative to where a manager remembers them.

- **Antetokounmpo at 30 minutes:** about 28.7 points, 10.2 reb, 5.6 ast. That is close to his 2025-26 per-game output, because 30 minutes is close to the 28.9 he was already playing. The trade barely touches his box score if the minutes hold.
- **Adebayo at 29 minutes:** about 18.0 points, 8.9 reb, 2.9 ast. That is a real step down from his 20.1 points and 10.0 rebounds a year ago, because he is losing 3.4 minutes off a role where he was the clear first offensive option, not sharing it with a former MVP.

The gap is the point. Antetokounmpo's minutes barely move under this plan, since he is stepping into a role close to the one he already had. Adebayo's minutes and his shot of the offense both shrink, since Miami now runs through Antetokounmpo when both are healthy.

## Why Bam, and not Giannis, absorbs the hit

Two players who each want the ball on the block and each want defensive rebounds cannot both get their usual share. Antetokounmpo arrives as a three-time MVP and the better individual scorer of the two (34.4 points per 36 against 22.3), so the offense tilts toward him. Adebayo's rebounding rate barely moved from last year (11.1 per 36 now against 10.7 for his career), which says the skill is intact. What changes is how many of Miami's live rebounds are still on the board for him to grab once Antetokounmpo is also crashing the glass.

Antetokounmpo's own weak spot, a 65.0% free-throw mark, does not improve with the trade. If his free-throw rate holds, more shot volume in a better offense could mean more trips to the line at that same percentage, which is a real cost in nine-category free-throw-percentage leagues even while his counting stats stay strong.

## The decision

If Antetokounmpo is on the board in the range you had him penciled in before the trade, take him there. The minutes math says his role survives the move. If Adebayo is on the board at his old price, in the range for a top-15 center, let him go and take the next name instead. He is still a good rebounder and a plus defender, but the 29-minute, second-option version of him is a different fantasy player than the 32-minute, first-option version the ranking sites remember.

## What would change this

A blowout preseason, an injury to either big, or Spoelstra abandoning the stagger plan in favor of playing them 15 to 20 minutes together a night would all move these numbers, in either direction. Recheck both players' minutes after the first two weeks of the regular season rather than trusting a September projection into November.
