Before you draft, check one setting: is your league points or categories? It moves [Giannis Antetokounmpo](/player/NBA/3032977) from 53rd on a nine-category board to seventh on a points board. It moves [Kel'el Ware](/player/NBA/5105623) the other way, from 47th to 103rd. Same players, same season, same numbers. Only the scoring changed.

The punting series and the games-missed column were all written for nine-category leagues. This one is for everyone else. **Formats:** head-to-head points, scored with the system ESPN made its default in 2020, set against head-to-head or roto nine-category. **Pool:** the same 150 players as the recent columns, the most nine-category value among the 247 NBA players who played at least 30 games and 20 minutes a night in 2025-26. Every figure is a 2025-26 per-game average from season totals, at that player's own minutes, and a player traded during the season counts his games for both teams. Nothing here is a projection of 2026-27. My assumption is that each player keeps last season's minutes and last season's rate.

## The scoring I used

ESPN's default points system gives:

- 1 for each point and 1 more for each made three
- 2 for each made field goal and minus 1 for each field goal attempt
- 1 for each made free throw and minus 1 for each free throw attempt
- 1 for each rebound, 2 for each assist, 4 for each steal and 4 for each block
- minus 2 for each turnover

If your league changes any of those weights, the ranks below change with them. Check your settings page before you trust my order.

Run it through a made two and you see the shape: 2 points, plus 2 for the make, minus 1 for the attempt, is 3. A missed shot costs 1. So a bad shooter loses a point here and there, but every attempt that goes in pays three times what the miss costs. Volume wins. In nine categories, field goal percentage and free throw percentage are two of the nine scores, each worth as much as all of a player's points. Efficiency is a ninth of the board each time, and a high-volume, low-percentage player pays for it twice.

## A worked example

Two hypothetical players, per game:

- **Player A**, a 22-minute center: 11 points on 5 of 8 shooting and 1 of 2 at the line, 9 rebounds, 1.5 assists, 0.6 steals, 1.5 blocks, 1 turnover. That scores 30.4.
- **Player B**, a 34-minute forward: 22 points on 8 of 18 with two threes and 4 of 5 at the line, 6 rebounds, 7 assists, 1 steal, 0.5 blocks, 3.5 turnovers. That scores 40.0.

In nine categories, A beats B in four categories (field goal percentage, turnovers, rebounds and blocks) and loses five. That is close. In points, B is ahead by 9.6 a night. Assists alone give B 11 more, and A claws a little back on the glass and with blocks.

## What barely changes

The order of the two boards agrees more than it disagrees. Four of the nine-category top five stay in the points top five, and [Victor Wembanyama](/player/NBA/5104157) only drops to fourth. Seven of the top 10 hold, 15 of the top 20 and 41 of the top 50. [Nikola Jokić](/player/NBA/3112335) is first on both, by more than seven points a game in points.

Giannis is the clearest case. In nine categories his free throw z-score (how many standard deviations he sits from the pool average) was -4.70, the biggest single drag on his rank. In points, his missed free throws cost him 3.5 a game out of 49.4. His assists alone are worth 10.9.

Castle makes the longest climb on the board, 85 places. Assists pay him 14.8 a game. His turnovers still cost him 6.4 and his missed free throws 1.5, but in points those are subtractions from a big number, not two of nine scores.

Two notes before you move Giannis up. The rank is per game, and he played 36 games last season, which is the problem the games-missed column priced. And it rests on 28.9 minutes a night. He scored 1.71 points a minute, third in the pool. At 26 minutes that is 44.4 a game and 12th on this board. At 32 minutes it is 54.7 and fifth.

Ware is the lesson. Per minute he is 22nd in the pool, at 1.30 points a minute. Per game he is 103rd, because he played 22.1 minutes. His nine-category value leaned on a positive turnover score (+1.41), blocks and shooting. In points, his low turnovers are worth about 2 a game over the pool average, his shooting about 1.3, and his blocks pay 4.4. None of that makes up for 22 minutes. If his minutes reach 28, the same rate is 36.4 a game and 49th. That minutes number is the whole decision, and I do not know it yet.

Anunoby and Bridges are the other type: 33 minutes a night and modest assists, 2.2 and 3.7 a game against a pool average of 3.8. Bridges' category value leaned on 1.0 turnovers a game. In points that is worth about 2 over an average player, the same as one extra assist. Anunoby's leaned on steals (+1.61), and steals do pay 4 each here, 6.2 a game, but 16.7 points and 2.2 assists leave him at 33.0 a game and 71st.

## What to do

- **Points league:** move Giannis into your top 10 if you believe the minutes, and push Banchero, Siakam, Avdija and Giddey up at least three rounds in a 12-team league. Move the centers who played 22 to 26 minutes (Ware, Williams, Queta) down 40 places or more.
- **Category league:** nothing here changes your board. Read the punting columns instead.
- **Either:** confirm your league's weights. A points league that cuts steals and blocks to 2 or adds a bonus for double-doubles is a different board, and you should rerun it.

The check to run in October is minutes. Ware, Clingan and Holmgren sit lower on this board because of the clock, not the rate, and preseason rotations are the first evidence of whether that changes.
