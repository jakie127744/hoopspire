Giannis Antetokounmpo finished 54th in nine-cat value last season.

Not 54th among centres. 54th overall, behind Kel'el Ware and Jalen Suggs, in a pool of 150 players. He had the largest field goal percentage impact of anyone in that pool, and it did not matter.

That number is not a provocation. It is arithmetic, and the arithmetic is the reason this desk exists.

## How this list was built

Every rank below comes from ESPN's 2025-26 per-game averages for all 30 rosters, run through the standard nine-cat method rather than through anyone's recollection of who is good.

The details, because a ranking without them is not checkable:

**The pool is 150 players.** A z-score measures distance from an average, and the average depends entirely on who is in the pool. Everyone with at least 30 games and 20 minutes a night was scored first, which gave 240 players. The top 150 of those were then rescored against each other, because 150 is roughly what a 12-team league actually rosters. Ranking against all 468 players with a season line would flatter every rotation regular and compress the top.

**Percentages are volume weighted.** A player's field goal contribution is his attempts multiplied by the gap between his percentage and the pool's aggregate percentage. That measures how far he moves a team number, which is the thing your league actually scores. It is the single most common error in a published list, and it is what puts Giannis where he is.

**Turnovers are inverted**, so a positive score means fewer of them.

**The sample floor is 30 games.** Several players in the top 50 cleared it barely, and their games played is in the table so you can discount them yourself. Nothing here is adjusted for injury, and nothing here knows anything about 2026-27.

## The Giannis line, category by category

Here is what produced 54th place. Raw averages on the left, the z-score each one earned on the right.

| Category | Giannis, per game | z-score |
|---|---:|---:|
| Points | 27.6 | +1.89 |
| Rebounds | 9.8 | +1.80 |
| Assists | 5.4 | +0.83 |
| Steals | 0.9 | -0.42 |
| Blocks | 0.7 | +0.10 |
| Threes made | 0.4 | -1.32 |
| Field goal impact | 10.4 of 16.6, 62.7% | +3.71 |
| Free throw impact | 6.4 of 9.9, 64.6% | -4.62 |
| Turnovers | 3.2 | -1.70 |
| **Nine-cat total** | | **+0.27** |

The field goal impact is the best mark in the entire pool. The free throw impact is the worst in the entire pool, ahead of Rudy Gobert at -3.35, and the two very nearly cancel.

That is the whole story. Shooting 64.6% from the line would be survivable on three attempts a game. On 9.9 attempts it drags a whole category down every week, and a category is a category whether you lose it by a point or by 40.

None of this says Giannis is a bad player, or even a bad fantasy player. It says he is a bad fantasy player *in nine-cat, in a lineup that intends to compete for free throw percentage*. Remove free throw percentage from the scoring entirely and he ranks 7th of 150, because the -4.62 stops being a cost and the +3.71 stays. This is the clearest case in the league of a rank that is useless without knowing the build it is going into.

In eight-cat, where turnovers come out, he moves to +1.97 and 34th, a gain of exactly 20 places. The player did not change.

## Where the scarcity actually is

The reason specialists rank higher than instinct says is that categories have different spreads, and a z-score is measured in spreads.

| Category | Pool average | Standard deviation | Spread relative to average |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Points | 16.71 | 5.75 | 0.34 |
| Rebounds | 5.81 | 2.22 | 0.38 |
| Assists | 3.74 | 1.99 | 0.53 |
| Steals | 1.05 | 0.34 | 0.33 |
| Blocks | 0.65 | 0.47 | 0.71 |
| Threes made | 1.70 | 0.98 | 0.58 |
| Turnovers | 1.87 | 0.78 | 0.42 |

Steals is the tightest category in the pool. Almost everybody is within a third of a steal of everybody else, so a player at two steals a game is a long way out in z-score terms without looking remarkable on a box score. That is why [Dyson Daniels](/player/NBA/4869342) at +2.77 in steals is a genuine asset and why nobody believes it until they see the spread.

Blocks runs the other way. It has the widest spread relative to its average, and [Victor Wembanyama](/player/NBA/5104157) at 3.1 per game produced a z-score of +5.23, the single largest category figure anywhere in the pool. The next best shot blocker, Alex Sarr, scored +2.88. More than half of Wembanyama's entire nine-cat total comes from that one category.

## What this list does not know

Four things, and they are the four that matter in September.

**It does not know about the offseason.** These are 2025-26 numbers, produced in last season's roles. [Hoopspire's 2026-27 projection](/story/NBA-2026-27-Projections) has Giannis in Miami, [Jaylen Brown](/player/NBA/3917376) and [LeBron James](/player/NBA/1966) in Philadelphia, [LaMelo Ball](/player/NBA/4432816) in Minnesota, [Ja Morant](/player/NBA/4279888) in Portland and [Kawhi Leonard](/player/NBA/6450) in Toronto. Every one of those players earned his rank in a different offence. Usage has to come from somewhere, and a ranking computed on history cannot tell you where.

**It does not know about minutes.** Wembanyama produced second place on 29.2 minutes a night. [Chet Holmgren](/player/NBA/4433255) produced 16th on 28.9. Both of those numbers could move by four in either direction this season, and four minutes is worth more than 20 places in the middle of this list. The minutes column is there so you can do that arithmetic yourself, which is the argument [the first column on this desk](/story/draft-the-minutes-not-the-names) made at length.

**It does not know about players who did not play.** [Tyrese Haliburton](/player/NBA/4396993) has no 2025-26 row in ESPN's record at all, so he cannot appear here at any rank. His most recent season is 2024-25, at 73 games and 33.6 minutes. A list built from last season will always show a missing player as absent rather than as cheap, and that gap is the most reliable value in a draft.

**It does not know about rookies.** No first-year player has a line to rank, which is not a flaw to correct. It is an accurate statement that the rate half of a rookie projection does not exist yet.

## Using it on draft day

Read the table in three passes.

First, find the gaps. The drop from +10.46 to +9.56 to +8.52 at the top is real separation. The stretch from 30th to 45th spans 0.98 in total, which is less than the gap between first and second, and treating those 16 players as ordered will cost you more than it gains. Draw tier lines where the numbers actually fall away.

Second, read the categories rather than the totals. Two players at the same total can be opposites, and your roster needs a shape, not a sum.

Third, apply your own minutes number to anyone whose team changed, then move him. That last step is the only one a published list can never do for you, and it is where the draft is won.

Giannis finished 54th. He is also the 7th most valuable player in the pool for a manager who builds around the number that put him there. Both statements come from the same nine rows of arithmetic, and a ranking that gave you only the first one would have told you almost nothing.
