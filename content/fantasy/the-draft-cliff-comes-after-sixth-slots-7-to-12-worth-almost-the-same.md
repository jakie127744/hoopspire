Here is the draft-slot rule for a 12-team snake draft: a top-six pick is worth chasing, and anything from seventh to 12th is one tier. If your league lets you pick a slot or trade picks before the draft, do not pay to move from 12th up to seventh. Do pay, within reason, to get into the top six.

**Format:** nine-category leagues, roto or head-to-head, 12 teams, snake draft. **Pool:** the same 150 players as our recent columns, the most nine-category value among NBA players who played at least 30 games and 20 minutes a night in 2025-26. **Minutes:** every player is valued at the per-game numbers he posted in 2025-26, so each one is assumed to play the minutes he played last season. Games missed are not priced in here. [Games missed: who moves](/story/games-missed-who-moves-embiid-butler-fall-barnes-durant-rise) covers that.

## How I measured it

A player's value is the sum of his nine z-scores. A z-score is how far he sits above or below the pool average in one category, measured in standard deviations. Then I did two things:

- **Gaps:** sorted the board and measured the drop from each player to the next one.
- **Slot totals:** added up what each draft slot collects in rounds one to four, assuming every manager takes the best player left on this board. In a snake draft the order reverses each round, so slot 1 picks 1st, 24th, 25th and 48th.

Real drafts do not follow one board. Treat the slot totals as the shape of the draft, not a forecast of your team. All values are rounded to two decimals, and the totals are added from the rounded values.

## The biggest gap on the board is after sixth

Across all 150 players, the average gap between neighbours is 0.09. The biggest gap is between [Luka Dončić](/player/NBA/3945274), sixth at 6.01, and [Donovan Mitchell](/player/NBA/3908809), seventh at 4.54. That is a drop of 1.47.

That one step is bigger than the whole spread from seventh to 12th. Mitchell is at 4.54 and [Scottie Barnes](/player/NBA/4433134), 12th, is at 3.79, a spread of 0.75.

The top six are spread out too, and each step is a real one:

- [Nikola Jokić](/player/NBA/3112335) 10.51
- [Victor Wembanyama](/player/NBA/5104157) 9.65
- [Shai Gilgeous-Alexander](/player/NBA/4278073) 8.57
- [Kawhi Leonard](/player/NBA/6450) 7.26
- [Tyrese Maxey](/player/NBA/4431678) 7.00
- Dončić 6.01

The six biggest gaps on the whole board all sit between players ranked first to eighth.

## What each slot collects in four rounds

| Slot | Total | Slot | Total |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 14.96 | 7 | 9.72 |
| 2 | 14.33 | 8 | 9.39 |
| 3 | 13.42 | 9 | 9.40 |
| 4 | 12.17 | 10 | 9.39 |
| 5 | 12.13 | 11 | 9.43 |
| 6 | 11.27 | 12 | 9.36 |

From slot 6 to slot 7 the total falls by 1.55, the biggest drop between two neighbouring slots. From slot 7 to slot 12 the totals sit within 0.36 of each other.

Here is why. Slot 7 gets Mitchell (4.54), then the 18th player, [Cade Cunningham](/player/NBA/4432166) (2.81), then the 31st (1.54) and the 42nd (0.83). Slot 12 gets Barnes (3.79) and [Anthony Edwards](/player/NBA/4594268) (3.47) back to back, then the 36th (1.09) and the 37th (1.01). The snake hands slot 12 back in round two what it lost in round one.

## What to do with it

- **Choosing a slot:** rank 1 to 6 in order, then treat 7 to 12 as one tier. If 7 to 12 is all that is left, choose on something else, such as whether you want two picks in a row at the turn of each round.
- **Trading picks before the draft:** moving from 12th to seventh gains you 0.36 over four rounds on this board, spread across four players. That is less than a quarter of the 1.55 between slots 6 and 7. Do not pay for it.
- **Moving into the top six:** slot 6 collects 1.55 more than slot 7. That is worth a real price.
- **Check the cliff against your own board.** It rests on last season's minutes and games. If your guess for one of the top six is lower, he may fall below the cliff.
