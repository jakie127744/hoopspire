Dyson Daniels ranks 36th of 150 in a nine-category pool. Remove steals from the scoring and he ranks 117th, a fall of 81 places, the largest move in this series so far. If you plan to punt steals, do not pay a top-40 price for him.

This column follows the earlier ones on blocks, free throws, turnovers and assists. It is about punting steals. Punting means giving up the category on purpose and building around the other eight. **Format:** nine-category head-to-head, where you win a week by taking five of nine categories. Punt steals and you need five of the remaining eight.

**Pool:** the same 150 players as the nine-cat top 50 and the earlier columns, taken from the 240 NBA players with at least 30 games and 20 minutes a night in 2025-26, then the top 150 by nine-category value. Every figure is a 2025-26 per-game average at that player's own minutes, from ESPN. Nothing here is a projection of 2026-27.

## How steals are spread

The pool averages 1.05 steals a game with a standard deviation of 0.34, the tightest spread of any counting category in this series. Only three of the 150 players average 2.0 or more. The ten best players in the nine-category ranking average 1.41 steals a game, barely above the pool.

Steals correlate 0.40 with assists and 0.29 with turnovers across the 150 players. Ball-handlers who create turnovers on defense tend to also cough the ball up on offense. Steals correlate -0.23 with blocks and -0.18 with rebounds, so punting the category pushes the draft toward big men the same way punting assists does, for a different reason: shot-blockers are not the ones gambling for steals.

## How the punt ranking is built

Each player has a z-score in each category, which measures how many standard deviations he sits from the pool average. To build the punt ranking I subtracted each player's steal z-score from his nine-category total and re-ranked the 150. I did not rebuild the pool without steals, so a player just outside the 150 could rank differently in a true punt draft. Read the ranks as a guide to direction and size, not as exact draft positions.

## What barely changes

The top holds more than in any earlier column here. Nikola Jokić, second in nine-cat, is first with steals removed. Victor Wembanyama, first in nine-cat, is second. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander stays third, Kawhi Leonard fourth and Tyrese Maxey fifth. Seventeen of the top 20 and 42 of the top 50 hold.

My reading is the same one from the assists column, scale. A player who leads in points, rebounds and shooting does not lose that lead by giving up 1.4 steals a game. The exception in this pool is a player whose value leans harder on one category than the others do, which is exactly who falls below.

## The minutes assumption

A steal total is a per-minute rate multiplied by a guess about minutes. Here is the arithmetic for a hypothetical guard, not a real player, who records 0.05 steals a minute.

- At 24 minutes a game: 1.20 steals, which is 0.15 above the pool average and about 0.45 standard deviations.
- At 32 minutes a game: 1.60 steals, which is 0.55 above the pool average and about 1.61 standard deviations.

The same player is worth about 1.16 standard deviations more in this category at 32 minutes than at 24, the largest minutes swing in this series relative to the category's own spread, because the whole pool sits so close together. A tight category means a small minutes change moves a player's standing by more, not less.

## What this does not say

This is 2025-26 data, one season of it, and it says nothing about 2026-27 roles. A player who moves to a new team, or into a bigger defensive role, changes his steal total, and this list changes with it. The pool was fixed before steals were removed, and a player's rank in a real punt draft depends on who else is available. Punting is a choice for a team you have already built, not a rule for every draft.

The finding that holds is the shape of the effect. At the top of the board it is small, smaller here than in any earlier column. In the middle of the board, the players whose value leans hardest on one thin category, a high steal rate on a modest scoring line, are the ones who fall the furthest when that category is removed.
