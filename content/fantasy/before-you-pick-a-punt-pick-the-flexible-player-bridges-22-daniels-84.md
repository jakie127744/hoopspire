Here is the rule for the early rounds this week: until you know which category you are punting, draft the player who holds his value in every build. When two names sit within a few places of each other on your board, take the one whose worst case is smaller.

**Format:** head-to-head nine-category. Punting, which means giving up one category on purpose to win the other eight, is a head-to-head plan. In roto every category counts toward the total, so this column is not for you.

**Pool:** the same 150 players as our recent columns, the most nine-category value among NBA players who played at least 30 games and 20 minutes a night in 2025-26. **Minutes:** every player is valued at the per-game numbers he posted in 2025-26, so each one is assumed to play the minutes he played last season. If your minutes guess for a player differs, [Four minutes either way](/story/four-minutes-either-way-jokic-moves-two-places-jay-huff-moves-105) shows how far that moves him.

## How I measured it

A player's value is the sum of his nine z-scores. A z-score is how far he sits above or below the pool average in one category, measured in standard deviations. To punt a category, you drop it and add up the other eight.

So I built ten boards: the normal nine-category board, and one board for each of the nine punts, with every player re-ranked with that category dropped. For each player I kept two numbers:

- **Worst case:** his rank in the punt build that hurts him most.
- **Worst-case drop:** how many places that is below his nine-category rank.

For 53 of the top 60, the build that hurts most is the punt of his best category. You would not punt steals after drafting Dyson Daniels. That is the point: an early pick like Daniels decides your build for you. A low worst-case drop means the player leaves it open.

## The more categories he helps, the less he can fall

Here is the worst-case drop for the top 60, grouped by how many categories each player is above average in:

| Categories above average | Players | Median worst-case drop |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 2 | 60 |
| 4 | 19 | 49 |
| 5 | 23 | 33 |
| 6 | 14 | 17 |
| 7 | 2 | 7.5 |

The pattern is steady. Every extra category a player helps in cuts the median drop.

The top of the board is safe whatever you do. None of the top six falls below 10th in any build. [Nikola Jokić](/player/NBA/3112335) is never worse than fourth.

The trouble starts in the second round. Across ranks 13 to 24 the median worst-case drop is 28.5 places. Across ranks 49 to 60 it is 53.

## Three close calls

These pairs sit close together on the nine-category board. The worst cases do not.

- **[Mikal Bridges](/player/NBA/3147657), 34th, or [Dyson Daniels](/player/NBA/4869342), 35th.** Bridges is above average in six categories, and his worst build, punting turnovers, drops him to 56th. His steals z-score, 2.76, is bigger than his whole nine-category total of 1.24. Punt steals and he falls to 119th, a drop of 84. Daniels also has the higher ceiling: punt points and he is 16th.
- **[Onyeka Okongwu](/player/NBA/4431680), 32nd, or [Jalen Duren](/player/NBA/4433621), 28th.** Okongwu's worst case is 45th. Duren's is 101st, in a build that punts field goal percentage. Punt threes instead and Duren jumps to 10th.
- **[Cooper Flagg](/player/NBA/5041939), 37th, or [Alex Sarr](/player/NBA/5160992), 43rd.** Flagg is above average in six categories and never falls below 56th. Sarr is above average in three. His blocks carry him, and a blocks punt sends him to 128th.

In every pair the specialist has the much lower worst case. In the first two, he also has the higher best case. That is the trade.

## What to do with it

- **Rounds one to four, build not set:** when two players are within about six places, take the one with more categories above average. Bridges over Daniels, Okongwu over Duren, Flagg over Sarr.
- **Once your build is set:** flip it. The specialists become the bargains, because your build drops the category that would hurt them. If you are punting threes, Duren at 28th on the standard board is a top-10 player for you. Our punting columns list who rises in each build.
- **Do not overpay for flexibility in the first round.** The top six hold their value in every build, so pick from them on value alone.

## The limits

Every number here is a 2025-26 per-game line held fixed. It assumes each player plays the minutes he played last season and keeps his per-minute rates, which a new role can change. A pool of 150 means 150th is the floor, which caps the biggest drops.

The check to run on your own board: next to each player in your top 60, write his number of categories above average. When the next two names are close, let that number break the tie.
