Giannis Antetokounmpo ranks 54th of 150 in a nine-category pool. Remove field goal percentage from the scoring and he ranks 144th, a fall of 90 places, the largest move in this series. If you plan to punt field goal percentage, do not draft him at all.

This column follows the earlier ones on blocks, free throws, turnovers, assists and steals. It is about punting field goal percentage. Punting means giving up the category on purpose and building around the other eight. **Format:** nine-category head-to-head, where you win a week by taking five of nine categories. Punt field goal percentage and you need five of the remaining eight.

**Pool:** the same 150 players as the nine-cat top 50 and the earlier columns, taken from the 240 NBA players with at least 30 games and 20 minutes a night in 2025-26, then the top 150 by nine-category value. Every figure is a 2025-26 per-game average at that player's own minutes, from ESPN. Nothing here is a projection of 2026-27.

## How field goal percentage is scored

A percentage is not a count, so it is scored by how much a player moves your team's number. The pool shoots 48.6% as a whole. For each player I multiplied his shots per game by the gap between his percentage and 48.6%. A 65% shooter on 12 shots moves your team more than a 70% shooter on six, and this measure says so.

Field goal impact moves against threes more than against any other category. Across the 150 players it correlates -0.69 with three-pointers made. It correlates 0.53 with rebounds and 0.32 with blocks. Most of the players who lift your field goal percentage are big men who rarely shoot from deep: 16 players in the pool averaged 0.0 threes a game.

It also moves against the other percentage. Field goal impact correlates -0.45 with free throw impact. That is why this column reads like the free throw column turned upside down. Giannis jumped 47 places when free throws were punted. Here he falls 90.

## How the punt ranking is built

Each player has a z-score in each category, which measures how many standard deviations he sits from the pool average. To build the punt ranking I subtracted each player's field goal z-score from his nine-category total and re-ranked the 150. I did not rebuild the pool without field goal percentage, so a player just outside the 150 could rank differently in a true punt draft. Read the ranks as a guide to direction and size, not as exact draft positions.

## What barely changes

Eight of the top ten hold. Victor Wembanyama moves from 2nd to 1st and Nikola Jokić from 1st to 2nd. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, who shoots 55.7% on 19.4 shots, drops from 3rd to 6th and is still sixth. Seventeen of the top 20 and 43 of the top 50 hold.

Two players leave the top ten, Jamal Murray (8th to 12th) and Lauri Markkanen (10th to 11th). James Harden (15th to 7th) and Stephen Curry (12th to 9th) come in.

LaMelo Ball is the one to circle. He moves from 41st to 18th, into the second round of a 12-team draft, because his 40.5% on 17.3 shots is the worst field goal impact in the pool. Derrick White, 25th to 13th, is the same case at a lower shot volume.

## The minutes assumption

Field goal impact is shots multiplied by the gap to 48.6%, and shots come from minutes. Here is the arithmetic for a hypothetical guard, not a real player, who shoots 42% and takes 0.40 shots a minute.

- At 24 minutes a game: 9.6 shots, an impact of -0.63, which is about 1.00 standard deviations below average.
- At 32 minutes a game: 12.8 shots, an impact of -0.84, which is about 1.34 standard deviations below average.

More minutes make a poor shooter hurt your field goal percentage more, not less. In a punt build that no longer matters, which is the whole point: you get his extra threes and points at 32 minutes and pay nothing for the misses.

The same arithmetic runs the other way for a hypothetical center who shoots 60% on 0.30 shots a minute. At 24 minutes he is about 1.30 standard deviations above average in this category, and at 32 minutes about 1.74. That is value you throw away when you punt, and the more he plays, the more you throw away.

## What this does not say

This is 2025-26 data, one season of it, and it says nothing about 2026-27 roles. Giannis played 36 games and changed teams this summer. A shooter whose role grows or shrinks moves up or down this list with his shot count. The pool was fixed before field goal percentage was removed, and a player's rank in a real punt draft depends on who else is available.

The finding that holds is the shape of the effect. At the top of the board it is small. In the middle, seven efficient centers who do not shoot threes fall 50 places or more, and the high-volume, low-percentage guards take their places. If you punt field goal percentage, stop drafting centers for their percentage, and draft the guards everyone else avoids for theirs.
