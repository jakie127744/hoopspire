Here is a draft-room decision you can make before your first pick: choose which categories you are building, not which players you like. The nine categories do not move on their own. Some come in bundles, and some fight each other. If you draft for threes, you are drafting away from field goal percentage, whether you meant to or not.

**Format:** head-to-head nine-category or roto. **Pool:** the same 150 players as the recent columns, the most nine-category value among the 247 NBA players who played at least 30 games and 20 minutes a night in 2025-26. Every figure is a 2025-26 per-game z-score at that player's own minutes, so nothing here is a projection of 2026-27.

## How I measured it

A **z-score** says how far a player sits above or below the pool average in one category, in standard deviations. For every pair of categories I measured the **correlation** across the 150 players: a number from -1 to +1. Near +1 means players who are good at one are usually good at the other. Near -1 means players good at one are usually bad at the other. Near zero means no pattern.

Turnovers are scored so that a high z-score means few turnovers. A negative correlation with turnovers therefore means the category comes with more turnovers.

## The pairs that matter

| Pair | Correlation |
|---|---:|
| Threes and Free throw % | +0.55 |
| Points and Assists | +0.54 |
| Rebounds and Field goal % | +0.52 |
| Points and Threes | +0.47 |
| Rebounds and Blocks | +0.46 |
| Assists and Steals | +0.39 |
| Rebounds and Free throw % | -0.44 |
| Field goal % and Free throw % | -0.45 |
| Rebounds and Threes | -0.45 |
| Threes and Field goal % | -0.68 |
| Points and Turnovers | -0.74 |
| Assists and Turnovers | -0.76 |

Three bundles stand out.

- **The big-man bundle: rebounds, blocks and field goal percentage.** Rebounds and field goal percentage come together at +0.52, rebounds and blocks at +0.46.
- **The shooter bundle: threes and free throw percentage,** at +0.55, the strongest positive pair. Points come with threes too, at +0.47.
- **The creator bundle: points, assists and steals,** with points and assists at +0.54. This one has a price. Points and assists are the two categories most tied to turnovers, at -0.74 and -0.76.

And the fights. Threes against field goal percentage is the strongest negative pair, -0.68. Field goal percentage against free throw percentage is -0.45, and rebounds against threes is -0.45.

## How few players beat the trade

I counted the players who sit at least half a standard deviation above average in both sides of a pair.

- **Threes and field goal percentage:** two players out of 150, [Kawhi Leonard](/player/NBA/6450) and [Kevin Durant](/player/NBA/3202).
- **Field goal percentage and free throw percentage:** six players: [Nikola Jokić](/player/NBA/3112335), [Victor Wembanyama](/player/NBA/5104157), [Shai Gilgeous-Alexander](/player/NBA/4278073), Leonard, Durant and [Jimmy Butler III](/player/NBA/6430). All six rank in the top 22 of the pool.
- **The whole big-man bundle** (rebounds, blocks and field goal percentage): 12 players, led by Wembanyama, [Chet Holmgren](/player/NBA/4433255) and [Alperen Şengün](/player/NBA/4871144).
- **The whole shooter bundle** (threes and free throw percentage): 19 players, among them [Stephen Curry](/player/NBA/3975), [Jamal Murray](/player/NBA/3936299) and [Lauri Markkanen](/player/NBA/4066336).
- **The whole creator bundle** (points, assists and steals): seven players, and six of them cost you in turnovers. Butler is the exception.

If you plan to win threes and field goal percentage in the same week, you need one of two players. Everyone else makes you choose.

## A worked example

Two hypothetical players, in z-scores per game:

- **Shooter S:** threes +1.5, free throws +1.5, field goal percentage -1.0, rebounds -0.5.
- **Big man M:** threes -1.5, free throws -1.0, field goal percentage +1.5, rebounds +1.5.

Now two builds of four players each.

- **Build A, two of each:** threes 0, free throws +1.0, field goal percentage +1.0, rebounds +2.0.
- **Build B, four shooters:** threes +6.0, free throws +6.0, field goal percentage -4.0, rebounds -2.0.

Build A is a little ahead in three categories and level in one. Build B is far ahead in two and behind in two. In head-to-head categories you need five wins out of nine in a week, and a big lead survives a bad week far more often than a small one does. That is why I would rather be Build B and add points and steals around it.

## What to do

- **Pick one bundle in the first three rounds,** and keep drafting the categories that come with it.
- **Do not chase threes and field goal percentage together** unless Leonard or Durant falls to you.
- **If you take a creator early,** expect to lose turnovers, or plan to punt them. The turnover column showed what that does to the board.
- **Your punt and your bundle should match.** A shooter build is the natural home for punting field goal percentage. A big-man build pairs with punting free throws and threes.

The check to run before your draft: count how many of your top 30 targets sit in the bundle you want. If it is only a handful, the bundle will run dry before you have built it.
