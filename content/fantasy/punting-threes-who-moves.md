Jalen Duren ranks 28th of 150 in a nine-category pool. Remove three-pointers from the scoring and he ranks 10th. Stephen Curry goes the other way, from 11th to 40th. If you plan to punt threes, take Duren in the first round and let someone else take Curry.

This column follows the earlier ones on blocks, free throws, turnovers, assists, steals and field goal percentage. It is about punting threes. Punting means giving up a category on purpose and building around the other eight. **Format:** nine-category head-to-head, where you win a week by taking five of nine categories. Punt threes and you need five of the remaining eight.

**Pool:** the same 150 players as the nine-cat top 50 and the earlier columns, taken from the 247 NBA players with at least 30 games and 20 minutes a night in 2025-26 (a player traded during the season counts both of his teams), then the top 150 by nine-category value. Every figure is a 2025-26 per-game average at that player's own minutes. Nothing here is a projection of 2026-27.

## How threes are scored

Threes are a count, so a player's score is simple. The pool averages 1.70 threes a game, with a standard deviation of 0.97. A player's z-score in threes is how many of those standard deviations he sits above or below 1.70. Curry, at 4.4 a game, is 2.78 above, the best in the pool.

Fifteen players in the pool averaged 0.0 threes. Each of them scores 1.75 below average, the lowest possible mark. When you punt threes, that penalty disappears, so every one of the 15 gains exactly 1.75 in total value. No other player gains more.

Threes move against three other categories. Across the 150 players, the threes z-score correlates -0.68 with field goal impact, -0.45 with rebounds and -0.32 with blocks. It moves with points (0.47) and free throw impact (0.55). So the players you gain are the big men who rebound and finish near the rim. This column is the field goal percentage column turned upside down.

## How the punt ranking is built

Each player has a z-score in each category, which measures how far he sits from the pool average in standard deviations. To build the punt ranking I subtracted each player's threes z-score from his nine-category total and re-ranked the 150. I did not rebuild the pool without threes, so a player just outside the 150 could rank differently in a true punt draft. Read the ranks as a guide to direction and size, not as exact draft positions.

## What barely changes

The top five do not move: Nikola Jokić, Victor Wembanyama, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, Kawhi Leonard and Tyrese Maxey. Luka Dončić, who averaged 4.0 threes, slips from 6th to 8th and stays in the first round.

Four players leave the top ten: Donovan Mitchell (7th to 13th), Jamal Murray (8th to 20th), Trey Murphy III (9th to 23rd) and Lauri Markkanen (10th to 17th). Scottie Barnes (12th to 6th), Kevin Porter Jr. (14th to 7th), Chet Holmgren (15th to 9th) and Duren (28th to 10th) come in. Sixteen of the top 20 and 41 of the top 50 hold.

Every player in that table gained the same 1.75. The ones who started lower rise further because the board is more crowded down there. Ranks 10 to 30 are 2.25 apart in total value. Ranks 80 to 100 are 0.75 apart. Gobert is the clearest case: 79th in nine-cat, 38th here, with the most rebounds and blocks of the group.

Duren is the one to circle. He goes from a third-round pick in a 12-team draft to the first round, because a 19.5-point, 10.5-rebound line was being held back by one category he never plays.

## The minutes assumption

Here is where minutes matter, and where they do not. Take a hypothetical wing, not a real player, who makes 0.10 threes a minute.

- At 24 minutes a game: 2.4 threes, a z-score of 0.72.
- At 32 minutes a game: 3.2 threes, a z-score of 1.54.

That is the value you give up when you punt. The more he plays, the more you give up, so a shooter whose minutes are going up is a worse fit for this build, not a better one.

A center who averages 0.0 threes scores -1.75 at 20 minutes and at 35. His threes penalty does not depend on his minutes at all. When you punt threes, his minutes feed only his other eight categories: rebounds, blocks and points, but also turnovers and free throws. Gobert shot 52.6% from the line last season, so more Gobert minutes hurt your free throw percentage too. For a punt-threes team, the minutes guess that matters is the center's, and you should read it against his free throw line.

## What this does not say

This is 2025-26 data, one season of it, and it says nothing about 2026-27 roles. Zubac played 48 games and Hartenstein 47. A center who starts shooting threes this season would lose some of this gain. The pool was fixed before threes were removed, and a player's rank in a real punt draft depends on who else is available.

The finding that holds is the shape of the effect. The top five do not care. Below them, the 15 players who never shoot threes all gain the same amount, and 11 of them land in the top 60. If you punt threes, pair it with field goal percentage and rebounds, draft the centers the rest of your league treats as late picks, and let the high-volume shooters go.
