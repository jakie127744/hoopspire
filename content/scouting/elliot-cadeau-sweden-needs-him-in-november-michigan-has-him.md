Last Friday in Ann Arbor, [Elliot Cadeau](/player/NCAAM/4869764) scored one point in the first half of an exhibition against Oregon State. Then he scored 26 in the second. Michigan won 98-93 in front of 10,091 fans, and Cadeau finished with 27 points and eight assists. He made four free throws in the last 70 seconds to close it out.

That was a warm-up game in October. It still tells you where Cadeau will spend the winter, and where he will not.

On 27 November, Sweden play Hungary in Stockholm in a World Cup qualifier they badly need to win. Michigan spend that week in Las Vegas at the Players Era, an early-season college tournament, and its final is on the 27th. Sweden's young point guard will be on the wrong side of the Atlantic, and nobody did anything wrong.

## A Brooklyn birth, a Swedish passport

Cadeau was born in Brooklyn on 4 September 2004 and grew up in West Orange, New Jersey. His mother is Swedish, from Skåne in the south. He holds both US and Swedish citizenship and speaks both languages.

Sweden found him early. He won gold with the under-16 team at the Baltic Sea Cup in 2020 and was the tournament MVP. In the summer of 2022, in Romania, he led Sweden to the FIBA U18 European Championship Division B title. He averaged 21.3 points, scored 36 in the 79-66 final against Denmark and was named MVP. The win put Sweden back in Division A, the top tier of European youth basketball, for the first time since 2016.

That event also settles his status. FIBA's rules, in Book 3 of its Internal Regulations, say a player born outside the country counts as a local if they hold a passport issued before their 18th birthday, or if FIBA finds significant links to the country. To play in Romania he needed a Swedish passport, and he was 17. So he is a local, and he does not take Sweden's one "restricted" slot, the place usually called the naturalised spot.

The choice is also made. In November 2021, at 17, he was called up to the senior team for the first time. He made his senior debut on 24 February 2023 in Frankfurt, four points in seven minutes against Germany. Under the same rules, a player who has been on a roster in a FIBA national team competition has chosen his country, and the narrow ways to change it do not fit a player with his record. Cadeau is Swedish for basketball, for good.

## Champion, then back in blue and yellow

Then he was away from the national team for three years, and became a much better player.

He spent two seasons at North Carolina and led the Atlantic Coast Conference with 231 assists in the second. Last season he moved to Michigan and started all 40 games for a team that went 37-3. In the Final Four he had 13 points, 10 assists and four steals as Michigan beat Arizona 91-73. In the final on 6 April he scored 19 against UConn, and Michigan won 69-63 for their first national title in 37 years. He was named the tournament's Most Outstanding Player.

He entered the NBA draft, pulled out and came back to Michigan for his senior year.

In between, Sweden got him back. Before the July window they were fourth in their four-team first round group, and only the top three would go on. Sweden beat Czechia 87-76 at Hovet in Stockholm on 3 July, then won 88-79 in Ljubljana against Slovenia on 6 July. Cadeau started both. His numbers were small, five points in the first game and seven in the second, but Sweden finished third and moved on.

## The four games that decide it

FIBA plays these qualifiers in windows of a few days, in November, February, July and August. Here is the pattern. In the four first round games played in November and December 2025 and in February and March 2026, Sweden went 1-3. Neither Cadeau nor [Pelle Larsson](/player/NBA/4601025), the Miami Heat guard, played in any of them. With both in July, Sweden went 2-0.

In August Larsson was back and Cadeau was not. The federation's squad release said he had started preseason with Michigan. Larsson scored 31 in an 86-81 win in Helsinki against Finland. Three days later France beat Sweden 84-69 in Stockholm, in front of 14,750 people, a national record.

That leaves Sweden 4-4 in Group L, level with Slovenia and Hungary, behind France at 7-1 and Finland at 5-3. The top three go to the 2027 World Cup in Qatar. Four games are left, and all four are in the college season:

- 27 November: Sweden v Hungary, Avicii Arena, Stockholm
- 30 November: France v Sweden, Dijon
- 25 February: Sweden v Finland
- 28 February: Hungary v Sweden

The day after the France game, Michigan play Louisville in Detroit. In February they host Maryland on the 26th, the day after Sweden play Finland. Only the Hungary game on the 28th has open days on both sides in Michigan's schedule, and the trip would mean a flight to Europe and back near the end of the Big Ten regular season. I would not ask Michigan for it.

## Plan for the team that will be there

This is the same problem we wrote about with the Philippines women and [Naomi Panganiban's March](/story/naomi-panganiban-did-her-gilas-paperwork-early-the-philippines-problem-is-march). The passport was never the issue. The calendar is. [Sumayah Sugapong was free for the Philippines in March only because Arizona's season ended early](/story/sumayah-sugapong-was-free-for-gilas-in-march-because-arizona-lost-early), and nobody should build a plan on a loss.

Sweden's version is harder, because the games left are the ones that decide the World Cup. FIBA's windows will not move. So the federation should stop treating Cadeau as a missing piece for November and treat the November roster as the team. The players the calendar leaves free for these four games are the ones to build around. They need the minutes and the trust now.

If they get Sweden to Qatar, Cadeau's senior season will be long over, and he arrives as the reward. That is the order.

Here is the line I expect Swedish fans to argue with: if Sweden qualify, the guards who did it in November and February should start in Qatar, and a Final Four Most Outstanding Player who missed those windows should have to win the job back.
