On 4 March in Kansas City, Arizona lost to Arizona State 54-51 in the first round of the Big 12 Tournament. Sumayah Sugapong scored 12 points, tied for the team lead. The loss ended Arizona's season at 12-18.

A week later the Philippines opened its first World Cup Qualifying Tournament in Villeurbanne, near Lyon. Sugapong was on the team, which Filipino fans call Gilas.

On Sunday my colleague Noor Haddad wrote that [Naomi Panganiban missed that tournament](/story/naomi-panganiban-did-her-gilas-paperwork-early-the-philippines-problem-is-march) because San Diego State was still playing. Sugapong is the other half of that story. Both guards grew up in San Diego. Both play for the same national team. In the same March, one was busy and one was free. The reason Sugapong was free was a losing season.

That is worth a second look, because it tells the federation something about how to plan.

## The paperwork

In 2022 Sugapong played for the Philippines at the FIBA Under-18 Women's Asian Championship in Bengaluru, India. That was Division B, the second tier of Asian youth basketball. She averaged 14 points, 5.4 rebounds and 3.2 assists over five games. In the bronze medal game she scored 27 points with nine steals, and the Philippines beat Samoa 84-68.

When she left UC San Diego for Arizona in 2025, Arizona's athletics department announced that she would spend the summer representing her home country, the Philippines, at the FIBA Women's Asia Cup in China. It was her first time with the senior team.

FIBA's rules on who counts as a local are in Book 3 of its Internal Regulations. I read the version in force as of 22 April 2026. A player born outside the country counts as a local if they hold, or once held, that country's passport issued before their 18th birthday, or if FIBA rules that they have significant links to the country. A player who meets neither test can still play, but only as the team's one "restricted" player, the spot people usually call the naturalised slot.

Sugapong played an under-18 FIBA event for the Philippines. To do that she had to be under 18 and hold a Philippine passport. So she counts as a local, and she does not use up that one restricted spot.

## From the Big West to the Big 12

At UC San Diego, in the Big West Conference, she was the Big West Freshman of the Year in 2023-24, at 14.0 points a game. The next season she started all 38 games, averaged 15.0 points and led the Big West in points, assists and steals. She was the Most Outstanding Player of the Big West Championship, and she led the Tritons to their first NCAA tournament in program history.

At Arizona last season she averaged 11.1 points, 4.1 rebounds and 2.2 assists in 29 games, with 27 starts. She scored 31 points at Utah, a career high.

Arizona, in the Big 12 Conference, backed the trip. "Our program, our University, and the city of Tucson are extremely proud that they're representing their countries on the national stage this summer," coach Becky Burke said when the school announced her Asia Cup trip, along with a teammate's trip with Spain.

## What she did in France

The Philippines lost its first four games in Villeurbanne, starting with a 115-66 loss to France. Then, on 17 March, it beat Colombia 74-59.

Sugapong came off the bench against Colombia and scored 21 points in 23 minutes. She made all nine of her free throws. Over the five games she averaged 13.2 points and 2.4 assists, second on the team in scoring behind Kacey Dela Rosa at 15.6.

"I think everybody is super happy, super proud to come out of this tournament with a win," Sugapong said after the Colombia game. She was thinking of the fans too, back home in the Philippines and in the States. "I think we did a lot for our country."

The Philippines played that week without Panganiban. It did not have to play it without both of its San Diego guards.

## The catch is Arizona getting better

Here is the uncomfortable part. Sugapong was free in March because Arizona finished 12-18 and went out in the first round. Burke is now in her second season of rebuilding the program. If that rebuild works, Arizona will play later into March. A team still playing in the Big 12 Tournament, or in the NCAA tournament, is unlikely to send a senior guard to France in the middle of it.

So the better Sugapong's college team does this season, the less likely the Philippines is to see her the next time FIBA puts a women's window in March.

That is not a reason to wish Arizona bad luck. It is a reason to stop treating March availability as one question with one answer.

## What I would do

Noor's advice on Sunday was to plan March events on the assumption that the US college players will not be there. I agree. I would add one step.

Every February, the federation should ask each college player's coach a direct question: when could your season end, and if it ends early, can she come? Sugapong shows the answer is sometimes yes. A guard who averaged 13.2 points at a World Cup qualifier is worth that phone call.

The Philippines should build every March roster as if its college players will be busy. Then it should keep a seat open for the ones whose season ends in time, and have their paperwork ready before the bracket comes out.
