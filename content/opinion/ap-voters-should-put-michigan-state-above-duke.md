I would move Michigan State above Duke in Monday's AP poll, and I would keep Duke above Illinois. Those are two votes, and one rule explains both. A loss to a better team should cost you less than a loss to a worse one, and a win over a ranked team that you lead from the first minute should count for more than the margin says.

On Tuesday night, No. 5 Michigan State beat No. 2 Duke 79-73 at the United Center. After a 2-2 tie about a minute in the Spartans [never trailed](/story/michigan-state-never-trail-79-73-win-over-duke-fears-11-assists). They led by 14 in the second half, and [Jeremy Fears Jr.](/player/NCAAM/4711255) had 18 points and 11 assists. Duke cut it to five twice, with 2:03 left and again at 0:12, and never to fewer.

## Why Michigan State goes up

The poll is supposed to rank who has done the most so far, and in November there is not much to rank. Before Tuesday neither team had played anyone in the top 25. Duke beat Army 84-50 and Coastal Carolina 113-52. Michigan State beat Quinnipiac 96-59 and Toledo 91-61. The only game either team has played against a ranked opponent is the one Michigan State won.

I do not need a model to separate them. The two teams played each other, and Michigan State won by six without ever being behind. If Duke stays ahead of Michigan State in the poll, then the poll is saying that a result that has just happened counts for less than a preseason opinion about how good Duke would be.

## Why Duke stays above Illinois

Illinois lost on Tuesday too. The Illini, ranked No. 3, [lost 84-78 at No. 16 Texas Tech](/story/texas-tech-hold-off-no-3-illinois-84-78-cruz-davis-20), also by six points. Duke lost by six points to the No. 5 team. Illinois lost to a team ranked 11 places below the one that beat Duke, and it lost in Texas Tech's building. Both are fair reasons to rank Duke higher.

Illinois also gave the game away in specific ways. It shot 8 of 19 from the free-throw line while Texas Tech made 23 of 26. It made 10 of 34 threes. Those numbers do not look like a team that was outplayed everywhere. They look like a team that did not take care of what it controls.

## The other side

The case for leaving Duke at No. 2 is not foolish. It is one game, and the margin was six points. Duke shot 7 of 29 from three and 20 of 60 overall, and it had 18 offensive rebounds to Michigan State's nine. Some of that is the kind of night a good team has.

But I would not lean too hard on it. Over its first three games Duke is 22 of 73 from three, 30 percent, and in two of the three games it made 18 and 24 percent. Against Army it made 4 of 22. That is not a bad night. That is a pattern, and one worth watching.

Voters can be tempted to protect the team that was No. 2 last week, because moving it feels like punishment. I think that is the wrong way to think about a ballot. It is not a punishment. It is a list of who has played best.

## What the voters should do

Prediction, labelled as one: Michigan State will be ranked above Duke in the November 16 poll. What would prove me wrong: Duke still ahead.

If I had a ballot, my top three would be Florida, Michigan State and Duke, with Illinois below Duke. Florida won at Florida State 81-72, which is not a statement, but it did not lose. Michigan State beat a top-two team. Duke lost once, to that team, by six.

That is what a ballot is for. The voters should pick on results, and make the two moves this week: Michigan State up, Illinois below Duke.
