On October 25 I wrote that the PBA [should drop the twice-to-beat in every conference](/story/pba-twice-to-beat-should-go-every-conference-tim-cone). The Governors' Cup quarterfinals are over, and the best evidence I could ask for is Converge. Rain or Shine won Game 1 of that series. Under the twice-to-beat, that would have been the end of it.

## What happened

The best-of-five quarterfinals are done, and the lower seeds won three of the four. I wrote then that at least one would. TNT, the fourth seed in Group A, [won every game against Magnolia](/story/tnt-win-every-quarter-beat-magnolia-92-70-game-1-hollis-jefferson-26), the top seed in Group B, and swept it 3-0. Ginebra, the third seed in Group B, [finished off San Miguel in Game 5](/story/ginebra-close-out-san-miguel-110-94-game-5-murphy-31). NLEX, the top seed in Group A, [beat Phoenix](/story/deonte-burton-27-late-blocks-nlex-hold-off-phoenix-97-92-game-1) 3-0.

Then there is Converge. It was the third seed in Group A, and it drew the second seed in Group B, Rain or Shine. Rain or Shine won Game 1 on October 27. Converge won the next three, the last one 120-109 on November 6, and reached the first semifinal in the franchise's history since it joined the league in 2022.

## What would have been different

Here is the part I owe you, because it cuts against me. If the twice-to-beat had been in place, three of the four series would have gone the same way. NLEX would have won once and gone through. TNT would have won twice in a row, which it did, and gone through. Ginebra would have beaten San Miguel in Games 1 and 2, and gone through.

Only one result changes. Rain or Shine, the higher seed, would have needed to win once, and it did in Game 1. Converge would have been out after one game. The Final Four would have had Rain or Shine instead.

That is one series in four, and I do not want to dress it up. The twice-to-beat would have been mostly harmless. But the one it would have changed was the one that gave the league a new semifinalist.

## What the seed was worth

Look at how Rain or Shine got to be the higher seed. It finished second in Group B at 6-4. Converge finished third in Group A at 8-4. Rain or Shine won 60 percent of its games and Converge won 67 percent, so by win percentage Rain or Shine was the weaker of the two. The groups are different sizes, so the records are not the same measure, and that is exactly the problem. A twice-to-beat is a reward handed out by a table that cannot compare the teams it is rewarding.

There is also a smaller cost. The four series took 15 games. Under the twice-to-beat they would have taken six. Nine games would not have been played.

## The other side

I will give the other side its due. A one-game edge is a clear reward for a long group stage, and it saves the higher seed from a long series. A format has to respect the group stage somehow.

My answer is that the best-of-five already does. NLEX, a top seed, swept its series. Magnolia, the other top seed, lost in three games to TNT, the defending champion. That is honest. The group stage gets you the draw, and the series decides the rest.

## What the PBA should do

Prediction, labelled as one: Converge wins at least one game against NLEX in the semifinals. What would prove me wrong: NLEX sweeps.

The PBA should take the twice-to-beat out of the Philippine Cup and the Commissioner's Cup before next season, and give every quarterfinal five games. A seed is a guess about the future. A series is the answer. Stop paying out on the guess.
